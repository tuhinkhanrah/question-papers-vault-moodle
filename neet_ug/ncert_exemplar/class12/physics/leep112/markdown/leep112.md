12.28 The inverse square law in electrostatics is $|\mathbf{F}| = \frac{e^2}{(4\pi\varepsilon_0)r^2}$ for the

force between an electron and a proton. The $\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)$ dependence of

$|\mathbf{F}|$ can be understood in quantum theory as being due to the fact that the 'particle' of light (photon) is massless. If photons had a mass $m_p$, force would be modified to

$$
|\mathbf{F}| = \frac{e^2}{(4\pi\varepsilon_0)r^2} \left[ \frac{1}{r^2} + \frac{\lambda}{r} \right] \exp(-\lambda r) \text{ where } \lambda = m_p c / \hbar \text{ and } \hbar = \frac{h}{2\pi}.
$$

Estimate the change in the ground state energy of a H-atom if $m_p$ were $10^{-6}$ times the mass of an electron.
