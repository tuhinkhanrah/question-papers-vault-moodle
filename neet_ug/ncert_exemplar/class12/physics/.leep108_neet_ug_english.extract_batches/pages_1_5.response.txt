  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Given Data and Conditions</strong><br/>
Energy flux (intensity): \(\Phi = 20\text{ W/cm}^2\).<br/>
Surface area: \(A = 30\text{ cm}^2\).<br/>
Duration of exposure: \(t = 30\text{ minutes} = 30 \times 60\text{ s} = 1800\text{ s}\).<br/>
Speed of light in vacuum: \(c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}\).<br/>
Condition: completely non-reflecting surface (total absorption).</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Assumptions and Physical Model</strong><br/>
Radiation impinges at normal incidence. For a totally absorbing surface, the entire incident radiant energy is absorbed, and each increment of absorbed energy \(U\) delivers linear momentum \(p = \frac{U}{c}\).</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Relevant Formulae</strong><br/>
Total radiant energy incident on the surface:<br/>
\[U = \Phi \cdot A \cdot t\]
Total momentum transferred upon complete absorption:<br/>
\[p = \frac{U}{c}\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Step-by-Step Working</strong><br/>
Compute the total incident radiant power:<br/>
\[P = \Phi \cdot A = (20\text{ W/cm}^2) \times (30\text{ cm}^2) = 600\text{ W}\]
Compute the total energy delivered over \(1800\text{ s}\):<br/>
\[U = P \cdot t = 600\text{ W} \times 1800\text{ s} = 1.08 \times 10^6\text{ J}\]
Calculate the transferred momentum:<br/>
\[p = \frac{1.08 \times 10^6\text{ J}}{3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}} = 0.36 \times 10^{-2}\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s} = 36 \times 10^{-4}\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\]
Equivalently, expressed in scientific notation, this equals \(3.6 \times 10^{-3}\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\), which matches the numerical value \(36 \times 10^{-4}\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\).</p>
<p><strong>Step 5: Check and Final Answer</strong><br/>
Dimensional verification: \(\frac{\text{J}}{\text{m/s}} = \frac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}}{\text{m/s}} = \text{N}\cdot\text{s} = \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\). The units and order of magnitude are fully consistent.<br/>
Final Answer: \(36 \times 10^{-4}\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\).</p>]]></text>

An electromagnetic wave in free space is a transverse wave in which the electric field vector \(\mathbf{E}\), the magnetic field vector \(\mathbf{B}\), and the propagation vector \(\mathbf{k}\) form an orthogonal right-handed system.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Poynting Vector and Energy Flow</strong><br/>
The energy flow per unit area per unit time carried by an electromagnetic wave is defined by the Poynting vector:<br/>
\[\mathbf{S} = \frac{1}{\mu_0}(\mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{B})\]

<p><strong>Step 3: Evaluation of Vector Directions</strong><br/>
Because electromagnetic waves are transverse, both \(\mathbf{E}\) and \(\mathbf{B}\) oscillate perpendicular to the direction of motion, ruling out propagation along either individual field vector.<br/>

<p><strong>Step 4: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
The direction of propagation of the electromagnetic wave is directed along the vector cross product \(\mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{B}\).<br/>
Final Answer: \(\mathbf{E} \times \mathbf{B}\).</p>]]></text>

Close to the antenna (\(r \ll \lambda\)), the electrostatic dipole field dominates and falls off as \(\frac{1}{r^3}\), but this near-field component does not transport energy away.<br/>
In the radiation zone (far-field, \(r \gg \lambda\)), the outgoing wave carries power permanently away to infinity.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Relevant Formulae and Principles</strong><br/>
By conservation of energy, the total time-averaged power \(P\) passing through any closed spherical surface of radius \(r\) centered at the antenna must be independent of \(r\):<br/>
\[P = \oint I\,dA = I(r) \cdot 4\pi r^2 = \text{constant}\]
Intensity of a wave in terms of its electric field amplitude:<br/>
\[I \propto E_0^2\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Step-by-Step Working</strong><br/>
From power conservation across spherical wavefronts:<br/>
\[I(r) \propto \frac{1}{r^2}\]
Substitute \(I \propto E_0^2\):<br/>
\[E_0^2 \propto \frac{1}{r^2}\]
Take the square root on both sides:<br/>
\[E_0 \propto \frac{1}{r}\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 5: Check and Final Answer</strong><br/>
If the electric field fell as \(\frac{1}{r^2}\), the intensity would drop as \(\frac{1}{r^4}\), causing the total power crossing a sphere of radius \(r\) to vanish as \(r \to \infty\), which would prevent energy transport to the far field. Hence, the radiation electric field must fall off as \(\frac{1}{r}\).<br/>
Final Answer: \(\frac{1}{r}\).</p>]]></text>

  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principles</strong><br/>
An electromagnetic wave of intensity \(I\) delivers energy per unit area per unit time equal to \(I\).<br/>
Linear momentum per unit volume associated with the wave is \(\frac{I}{c^2}\), so the momentum delivered per unit area per second is \(\frac{I}{c}\).<br/>
Radiation pressure \(p\) equals the rate of momentum transferred per unit area.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Case Analysis</strong><br/>
- Completely absorbing surface: The incident radiation is fully absorbed, so the initial momentum flux \(\frac{I}{c}\) is brought to rest. The momentum transferred per unit area per unit time is:<br/>
\[p = \frac{I}{c}\]<br/>
- Completely reflecting surface: The incident wave with momentum flux \(\frac{I}{c}\) is reflected back with momentum flux \(-\frac{I}{c}\). The change in momentum per unit area per unit time is:<br/>
\[p = \frac{I}{c} - \left(-\frac{I}{c}\right) = \frac{2I}{c}\]<br/>
- Real surfaces: Real physical surfaces are neither perfectly absorbing nor perfectly reflecting; they have a reflection coefficient \(r\) such that \(0 < r < 1\). The resulting radiation pressure is:<br/>
\[p = (1 + r)\frac{I}{c}\]<br/>
which lies strictly within the intermediate range \(\frac{I}{c} < p < \frac{2I}{c}\).</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Radiation pressure is \(I/c\) if the wave is totally absorbed, \(2I/c\) if the wave is totally reflected, and lies in the range \(I/c < p < 2I/c\) for real surfaces.<br/>
Final Answer: Radiation pressure is \(I / c\) if the wave is totally absorbed; radiation pressure is \(2I / c\) if the wave is totally reflected; and radiation pressure is in the range \(I / c < p < 2I / c\) for real surfaces.</p>]]></text>
