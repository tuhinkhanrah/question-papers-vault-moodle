  </name>
  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p>Three waves A, B and C of frequencies \(1600\text{ kHz}\), \(5\text{ MHz}\) and \(60\text{ MHz}\), respectively are to be transmitted from one place to another. Which of the following is the most appropriate mode of communication:</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>Different frequency bands of electromagnetic waves use different propagation modes based on how they interact with the Earth's surface and atmosphere. Ground (surface) wave propagation is limited to frequencies up to about \(1.5\text{ to }2\text{ MHz}\) due to severe attenuation caused by absorption by the Earth at higher frequencies. Sky wave propagation utilizes reflection by the ionosphere and is effective in the frequency range of approximately \(3\text{ MHz}\) to \(30\text{ MHz}\). Waves with frequencies greater than about \(30\text{ to }40\text{ MHz}\) penetrate through the ionospheric layers without being reflected back to Earth, so they must be transmitted via space wave (line-of-sight or satellite) propagation.</p>
<p><strong>Evaluation</strong><br/>The three given wave frequencies are evaluated according to their propagation mechanisms:<br/>
1. Wave A has a frequency of \(1600\text{ kHz} = 1.6\text{ MHz}\), which falls within the frequency band suitable for ground wave propagation.<br/>
2. Wave B has a frequency of \(5\text{ MHz}\), which lies in the short-wave range reflected by the ionosphere, making it suitable for sky wave propagation.<br/>
3. Wave C has a frequency of \(60\text{ MHz}\), which exceeds the critical frequency of the ionosphere and penetrates it, necessitating transmission via space wave propagation.</p>
<p><strong>Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Wave A is transmitted via ground wave, wave B via sky wave, and wave C via space wave.</p>]]></text>

  </name>
  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p>A \(1\text{ kW}\) signal is transmitted using a communication channel which provides attenuation at the rate of \(-2\text{ dB}\) per km. If the communication channel has a total length of \(5\text{ km}\), the power of the signal received is [gain in \(\text{dB} = 10\log \left(\frac{P_0}{P_i}\right)\)]</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Given Data and Conditions</strong><br/>Transmitted signal power: \(P_i = 1\text{ kW} = 1000\text{ W}\)<br/>
Rate of attenuation: \(-2\text{ dB/km}\)<br/>
Total length of the communication channel: \(d = 5\text{ km}\)</p>
<p><strong>Assumptions and Sign Conventions</strong><br/>Attenuation is treated as a negative gain in decibels. The total attenuation over the channel is the product of the attenuation rate per kilometer and the channel distance in kilometers.</p>
<p><strong>Relevant Formulae and Principles</strong><br/>Total gain in decibels:
\[\text{Gain (in dB)} = (\text{rate of attenuation}) \times d\]
The decibel gain is related to input power \(P_i\) and output power \(P_0\) by:
\[\text{Gain (in dB)} = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{P_0}{P_i}\right)\]</p>
<p><strong>Step-by-Step Working</strong><br/>1. Calculate total decibel gain:
\[\text{Total Gain} = -2\text{ dB/km} \times 5\text{ km} = -10\text{ dB}\]
2. Substitute into the logarithmic gain equation:
\[-10 = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{P_0}{1000}\right)\]
\[-1 = \log_{10}\left(\frac{P_0}{1000}\right)\]
3. Take the antilogarithm:
\[\frac{P_0}{1000} = 10^{-1} = 0.1\]
\[P_0 = 1000 \times 0.1 = 100\text{ W}\]
</p>
<p><strong>Check and Final Answer</strong><br/>A loss of \(10\text{ dB}\) corresponds to a reduction in signal power by a factor of 10. Thus, \(1000\text{ W} / 10 = 100\text{ W}\), confirming the received power is \(100\text{ W}\).</p>]]></text>

  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>In amplitude modulation, the instantaneous amplitude of the high-frequency carrier wave is varied in proportion to the instantaneous amplitude of the modulating (message) signal. Crucially, the frequency of the carrier oscillation remains unchanged during this process.</p>
<p><strong>Evaluation</strong><br/>Mathematically, the amplitude modulated wave is described by:
\[c_m(t) = [A_c + A_m \sin(\omega_m t)] \sin(\omega_c t)\]
Expanding the product yields:
\[c_m(t) = A_c \sin(\omega_c t) + \frac{\mu A_c}{2} \cos[(\omega_c - \omega_m)t] - \frac{\mu A_c}{2} \cos[(\omega_c + \omega_m)t]\]
The fundamental oscillation frequency of the modulated wave corresponds to the carrier frequency \(\omega_c\), around which the sidebands \(\omega_c \pm \omega_m\) are symmetrically distributed.</p>
<p><strong>Final Conclusion</strong><br/>The frequency of the amplitude modulated wave remains \(\omega_c\).</p>]]></text>

\[c_m(t) = \{A_c + k_2 v_m(t)\} \sin(\omega_c t + \phi)\]
Expressions that place the message signal inside the sine argument modulate frequency or phase rather than amplitude.</p>
<p><strong>Final Conclusion</strong><br/>The correct mathematical expression for the amplitude modulated wave is \(\{A_c + k_2 v_m(t)\} \sin(\omega_c t + \phi)\).</p>]]></text>

    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Given Data and Conditions</strong><br/>Frequency of the audio signal: \(\nu = 15\text{ kHz} = 15 \times 10^3\text{ Hz}\)<br/>
Speed of light in vacuum: \(c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}\)</p>
<p><strong>Assumptions and Sign Conventions</strong><br/>Transmission of baseband signals directly through free space is evaluated based on antenna dimension constraints, effective radiated power, and atmospheric propagation limitations.</p>
<p><strong>Relevant Formulae and Principles</strong><br/>1. Wavelength of the electromagnetic wave:
\[\lambda = \frac{c}{\nu}\]
2. Minimum required antenna dimension:
\[l \approx \frac{\lambda}{4}\]
3. Effective power radiated by a linear antenna of length \(l\):
\[P \propto \left(\frac{l}{\lambda}\right)^2\]
4. Ionospheric propagation: The ionosphere reflects frequencies typically between \(3\text{ MHz}\) and \(30\text{ MHz}\); baseband audio signals suffer heavy attenuation in the atmosphere and cannot propagate via sky waves.</p>
<p><strong>Step-by-Step Working</strong><br/>1. Calculate the wavelength for \(\nu = 15\text{ kHz}\):
\[\lambda = \frac{3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}}{15 \times 10^3\text{ Hz}} = 2 \times 10^4\text{ m} = 20\text{ km}\]
2. Compute the minimum required antenna size:
\[l \approx \frac{\lambda}{4} = \frac{20\text{ km}}{4} = 5\text{ km}\]
Constructing a vertical antenna of height at least \(5\text{ km}\) is practically infeasible.<br/>
3. If an antenna of length substantially smaller than \(5\text{ km}\) is used (\(l \ll \lambda\)), the radiated power \(P \propto (l/\lambda)^2\) becomes negligibly small, preventing long-distance transmission.<br/>
4. Moreover, audio frequency waves cannot be transmitted via sky wave reflection through the ionosphere.</p>
<p><strong>Check and Final Answer</strong><br/>Long-distance unmodulated transmission is prevented because the required antenna size is at least \(5\text{ km}\), the audio signal cannot propagate via sky waves, and the effective radiated power is extremely small if the antenna size is less than \(5\text{ km}\).</p>]]></text>

    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>The modulation index \(m\) in amplitude modulation is defined as the ratio of the peak amplitude of the modulating message signal \(A_m\) to the peak amplitude of the carrier signal \(A_c\):
\[m = \frac{A_m}{A_c}\]</p>
<p><strong>Evaluation</strong><br/>When \(m > 1\), the amplitude of the message signal exceeds the amplitude of the carrier wave (\(A_m > A_c\)). This condition is known as overmodulation:<br/>
1. During the negative peaks of the modulating wave, the envelope of the modulated carrier dips below zero and becomes severely clipped and distorted. As a result, the original modulating signal cannot be faithfully recovered by simple envelope detectors.<br/>
2. Overmodulation generates extraneous high-order harmonic frequencies that cause spectral splatter and severe overlapping of adjacent sidebands, leading to irreversible loss of transmitted information.</p>
<p><strong>Final Conclusion</strong><br/>A modulation index \(m > 1\) indicates that the amplitude of the message signal is greater than the amplitude of the carrier signal resulting in distortion, and it causes overlapping of both sidebands resulting in loss of information.</p>]]></text>
