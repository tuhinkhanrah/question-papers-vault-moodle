    <questiontext format="html">
      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Assertion :</strong> Molarity of a solution in liquid state changes with temperature.<br/><strong>Reason :</strong> The volume of a solution changes with change in temperature.</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>বিবৃতি (Assertion) :</strong> তরল দশায় কোনো দ্রবণের মোলারিটি তাপমাত্রার সাথে পরিবর্তিত হয়।<br/><strong>যুক্তি (Reason) :</strong> তাপমাত্রা পরিবর্তনের সাথে সাথে দ্রবণের আয়তন পরিবর্তিত হয়।</p>]]></text>

    <generalfeedback format="html">
      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle of Molarity</strong><br/>
Molarity is defined as the number of moles of solute dissolved per litre of solution:
\[M = \frac{\text{moles of solute}}{\text{volume of solution in litres}}\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Temperature Dependence of Volume</strong><br/>
The volume of a liquid expands upon heating and contracts upon cooling, meaning volume is directly dependent on temperature. In contrast, the mass of the solute and the number of moles of solute remain constant regardless of temperature changes. Because molarity is inversely proportional to the volume of the solution, any change in temperature results in a corresponding change in molarity.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
The assertion correctly states that molarity changes with temperature, and the reason correctly explains that this change occurs because the volume of the solution is temperature-dependent. Therefore, both assertion and reason are correct statements and reason is the correct explanation for assertion.</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>ধাপ ১: মোলারিটির ধারণাগত নীতি</strong><br/>

\[M = \frac{\text{দ্রাবের মোল সংখ্যা}}{\text{লিটারে দ্রবণের আয়তন}}\]</p>
<p><strong>ধাপ ২: আয়তনের উপর তাপমাত্রার প্রভাব</strong><br/>
তাপমাত্রা বৃদ্ধি পেলে তরল প্রসারিত হয় এবং তাপমাত্রা হ্রাস পেলে সংকুচিত হয়, অর্থাৎ দ্রবণের আয়তন তাপমাত্রার উপর নির্ভরশীল। কিন্তু দ্রাবের ভর বা মোল সংখ্যা তাপমাত্রার সাথে পরিবর্তিত হয় না। যেহেতু মোলারিটি দ্রবণের আয়তনের ব্যস্তানুপাতিক, তাই তাপমাত্রা পরিবর্তনের সাথে সাথে দ্রবণের মোলারিটিও পরিবর্তিত হয়।</p>

      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Intermolecular Forces and Volatility</strong><br/>
Methyl alcohol (boiling point \(64.7^\circ\text{C}\)) is significantly more volatile than water (boiling point \(100^\circ\text{C}\)). When methyl alcohol is added to water, the total vapour pressure over the solution becomes higher than the vapour pressure of pure water at the same temperature. As a result, the solution boils at a temperature lower than the boiling point of pure water, causing a depression rather than an elevation in boiling point.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Principles of Boiling Point Elevation</strong><br/>
Elevation in boiling point is a colligative property that is observed specifically when a non-volatile solute is dissolved in a volatile liquid solvent. Adding a volatile solute to a volatile solvent increases the total vapour pressure and does not lead to classical elevation in boiling point.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
The assertion incorrectly states that the boiling point of water increases, and the reason incorrectly asserts that adding a volatile solute causes boiling point elevation. Therefore, assertion and reason both are incorrect statements.</p>

      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Freezing Point and Vapour Pressure Relationship</strong><br/>
The freezing point of a substance is the temperature at which the liquid and solid phases exist in dynamic equilibrium and possess the exact same vapour pressure. At the freezing point, the vapour pressure of the liquid solvent must equal the vapour pressure of the pure solid solvent.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Effect of Adding a Non-Volatile Solute</strong><br/>
When a non-volatile electrolyte such as sodium chloride is dissolved in water, solute particles occupy positions at the liquid surface, reducing the escaping tendency of water molecules and lowering the equilibrium vapour pressure of the liquid solution relative to pure liquid water.<br/>
Because the vapour pressure of the solution is lower, it intersects the sublimation vapour pressure curve of solid ice at a lower temperature than pure water does. Thus, the solution freezes at a lower temperature, resulting in a depression in freezing point.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
The assertion correctly identifies that adding sodium chloride depresses the freezing point of water, and the reason correctly explains that this depression is caused by the lowering of vapour pressure. Therefore, assertion and reason both are correct statements and reason is the correct explanation for assertion.</p>

      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Assertion :</strong> When a solution is separated from the pure solvent by a semi-permeable membrane, the solvent molecules pass through it from pure solvent side to the solution side.<br/><strong>Reason :</strong> Diffusion of solvent occurs from a region of high concentration solution to a region of low concentration solution.</p>

<p><strong>বিবৃতি (Assertion) :</strong> যখন একটি অর্ধভেদ্য পর্দা দ্বারা দ্রবণকে বিশুদ্ধ দ্রাবক থেকে পৃথক রাখা হয়, তখন দ্রাবক অণুগুলি বিশুদ্ধ দ্রাবকের দিক থেকে দ্রবণের দিকে পর্দার মধ্য দিয়ে অতিক্রম করে।<br/><strong>যুক্তি (Reason) :</strong> দ্রাবকের ব্যাপন উচ্চ গাঢ়ত্বের দ্রবণ থেকে নিম্ন গাঢ়ত্বের দ্রবণের দিকে ঘটে।</p>]]></text>

    <generalfeedback format="html">
      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Understanding the Process of Osmosis</strong><br/>
When a solution and a pure solvent are separated by a semipermeable membrane, the chemical potential (or concentration) of the solvent is higher on the pure solvent side than on the solution side. Consequently, there is a spontaneous net flow of solvent molecules across the semipermeable membrane from the pure solvent side to the solution side. This phenomenon is termed osmosis.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Diffusion of Solvent Molecules</strong><br/>
Diffusion describes the spontaneous movement of any chemical species from a region where its own chemical potential or concentration is higher to a region where it is lower. Pure solvent represents the highest possible concentration and chemical potential of solvent molecules, whereas a solution has a lower solvent concentration. Thus, solvent molecules diffuse naturally from a region of higher solvent concentration to lower solvent concentration. However, osmosis across a semipermeable membrane is driven by the chemical potential gradient across the membrane barrier specifically restricting solute passage, so general diffusion does not serve as the direct operational definition or complete mechanistic explanation of osmosis.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
The assertion correctly states the direction of solvent passage during osmosis, and the reason correctly describes the general direction of solvent diffusion. However, the reason is not the correct explanation for the assertion. Therefore, assertion and reason both are correct statements but reason is not the correct explanation for assertion.</p>
