    <questiontext format="html">
      <text><![CDATA[<p>4 L of 0.02 M aqueous solution of NaCl was diluted by adding one litre of water. The molality of the resultant solution is ________.</p>
<hr/>
<p>0.02 M NaCl-এর 4 L জলীয় দ্রবণকে এক লিটার জল যোগ করে লঘু করা হলো। উৎপন্ন দ্রবণের মোলালিটি হবে ________।</p>]]></text>
    </questiontext>
    <generalfeedback format="html">
      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Given Data and Conditions</strong><br/>
Initial volume of solution: \(V_1 = 4\text{ L}\)<br/>
Initial molarity of NaCl solution: \(M_1 = 0.02\text{ M}\)<br/>
Volume of water added: \(V_{\mathrm{w}} = 1\text{ L}\)<br/>
Total volume of resultant solution: \(V_2 = V_1 + V_{\mathrm{w}} = 4\text{ L} + 1\text{ L} = 5\text{ L}\)</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Assumptions and Principles</strong><br/>
Dilution formula: \(M_1 V_1 = M_2 V_2\)<br/>
In very dilute aqueous solutions, the density of water is approximately \(1\text{ kg/L}\). Therefore, the mass of 5 L of water is approximately \(5\text{ kg}\). For highly dilute solutions, molarity and molality are numerically virtually identical because the mass of dissolved solute is negligible compared to the total mass of the solvent.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Step-by-Step Calculation</strong><br/>
Number of moles of NaCl:
\[n = M_1 V_1 = 0.02\text{ mol/L} \times 4\text{ L} = 0.08\text{ mol}\]
Molarity of resultant solution:
\[M_2 = \frac{n}{V_2} = \frac{0.08\text{ mol}}{5\text{ L}} = 0.016\text{ M}\]
Mass of solvent (water):
\[m_{\mathrm{solvent}} \approx 5\text{ L} \times 1\text{ kg/L} = 5\text{ kg}\]
Molality of resultant solution:
\[m = \frac{\text{moles of solute}}{\text{mass of solvent in kg}} = \frac{0.08\text{ mol}}{5\text{ kg}} = 0.016\text{ mol kg}^{-1} = 0.016\text{ m}\]
</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Check and Final Answer</strong><br/>
The calculated molality matches 0.016.</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>ধাপ ১: প্রদত্ত তথ্য ও শর্তাবলী</strong><br/>
দ্রবণের প্রাথমিক আয়তন: \(V_1 = 4\text{ L}\)<br/>
NaCl দ্রবণের প্রাথমিক মোলারিটি: \(M_1 = 0.02\text{ M}\)<br/>
যোগ করা জলের আয়তন: \(V_{\mathrm{w}} = 1\text{ L}\)<br/>
লঘুকৃত দ্রবণের মোট আয়তন: \(V_2 = 4\text{ L} + 1\text{ L} = 5\text{ L}\)</p>
<p><strong>ধাপ ২: নীতি ও সূত্র</strong><br/>
লঘুকরণ সূত্র: \(M_1 V_1 = M_2 V_2\)<br/>
অত্যন্ত লঘু জলীয় দ্রবণের ক্ষেত্রে জলের ঘনত্ব প্রায় \(1\text{ kg/L}\) হওয়ায় 5 L জলের ভর প্রায় \(5\text{ kg}\)। দ্রাবের নগণ্য ভরের কারণে লঘু দ্রবণে মোলারিটি ও মোলালিটির মান প্রায় সমান হয়।</p>
<p><strong>ধাপ ৩: ধাপে ধাপে গণনা</strong><br/>
NaCl-এর মোল সংখ্যা:
\[n = 0.02\text{ mol/L} \times 4\text{ L} = 0.08\text{ mol}\]
লঘুকৃত দ্রবণের মোলারিটি:
\[M_2 = \frac{0.08\text{ mol}}{5\text{ L}} = 0.016\text{ M}\]
দ্রাবকের ভর:
\[m_{\mathrm{solvent}} \approx 5\text{ kg}\]
দ্রবণের মোলালিটি:
\[m = \frac{0.08\text{ mol}}{5\text{ kg}} = 0.016\text{ mol kg}^{-1} = 0.016\text{ m}\]
</p>
<p><strong>ধাপ ৪: চূড়ান্ত সিদ্ধান্ত</strong><br/>
অতএব, লঘুকৃত দ্রবণের মোলালিটি হলো 0.016।</p>]]></text>

<p>নিচে প্রদত্ত তথ্যের ভিত্তিতে সঠিক বিকল্পটি চিহ্নিত করো।</p><p><strong>তথ্য :</strong> মিথানলে অ্যাসিটোন যোগ করলে মিথানল অণুগুলির মধ্যকার কিছু হাইড্রোজেন বন্ধন ভেঙে যায়।</p>]]></text>

    <generalfeedback format="html">
      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle of Intermolecular Forces and Raoult's Law</strong><br/>
Pure methanol exhibits strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding among its molecules. When acetone is added to methanol, acetone molecules occupy spaces between methanol molecules and disrupt some of these hydrogen bonds.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Deviation and Azeotrope Formation</strong><br/>
Because the attractive interactions between unlike molecules (methanol–acetone) are weaker than the hydrogen bonding interactions between like molecules (methanol–methanol), the escaping tendency of both components increases. Consequently, the mixture exhibits a higher vapour pressure than expected from Raoult's law, displaying positive deviation from Raoult's law.<br/>
Binary solutions showing large positive deviations from Raoult's law exhibit a vapour pressure maximum at an intermediate composition, which directly corresponds to a boiling point minimum. Hence, they form a minimum boiling azeotrope.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Therefore, at a specific composition, the methanol-acetone mixture will form a minimum boiling azeotrope and will show positive deviation from Raoult's law.</p>
<hr/>

    <questiontext format="html">
      <text><![CDATA[<p>\(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) value for \(\mathrm{Ar(g)}\), \(\mathrm{CO}_{2}(\mathrm{g})\), \(\mathrm{HCHO}(\mathrm{g})\) and \(\mathrm{CH}_{4}(\mathrm{g})\) are 40.39, 1.67, \(1.83 \times 10^{-5}\) and 0.413 respectively.</p><p>Arrange these gases in the order of their increasing solubility.</p>
<hr/>
<p>\(\mathrm{Ar(g)}\), \(\mathrm{CO}_{2}(\mathrm{g})\), \(\mathrm{HCHO}(\mathrm{g})\) এবং \(\mathrm{CH}_{4}(\mathrm{g})\)-এর জন্য \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\)-এর মান যথাক্রমে 40.39, 1.67, \(1.83 \times 10^{-5}\) এবং 0.413।</p><p>এই গ্যাসগুলিকে তাদের দ্রাব্যতার ঊর্ধ্বক্রমানুসারে সাজাও।</p>]]></text>
    </questiontext>
    <generalfeedback format="html">
      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Relevant Principle</strong><br/>
According to Henry's law, the solubility of a gas in a liquid at a given temperature is related to its partial pressure by:
\[p = K_{\mathrm{H}} x \implies x = \frac{p}{K_{\mathrm{H}}}\]
where \(x\) represents the mole fraction of the gas in solution (solubility), \(p\) is the partial pressure of the gas, and \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) is Henry's law constant.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Relationship Between Solubility and Henry's Constant</strong><br/>
At a constant partial pressure, the solubility \(x\) is inversely proportional to Henry's law constant \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\). A higher \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) value signifies lower solubility, while a lower \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) value signifies higher solubility.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Comparison of Given Values</strong><br/>
The given \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) values in descending order are:
\[K_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathrm{Ar}) = 40.39 > K_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathrm{CO}_2) = 1.67 > K_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathrm{CH}_4) = 0.413 > K_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathrm{HCHO}) = 1.83 \times 10^{-5}\]
Since solubility is inversely related to \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\), the order of increasing solubility is:
\[\mathrm{Ar} < \mathrm{CO}_2 < \mathrm{CH}_4 < \mathrm{HCHO}\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Final Answer</strong><br/>
The correct sequence arranged in order of increasing solubility is \(\mathrm{Ar} < \mathrm{CO}_2 < \mathrm{CH}_4 < \mathrm{HCHO}\).</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>ধাপ ১: প্রাসঙ্গিক নীতি</strong><br/>
হেনরির সূত্র অনুসারে, নির্দিষ্ট তাপমাত্রায় তরলে কোনো গ্যাসের দ্রাব্যতা তার আংশিক চাপের সাথে সম্পর্কিত:
\[p = K_{\mathrm{H}} x \implies x = \frac{p}{K_{\mathrm{H}}}\]
যেখানে \(x\) হলো দ্রবণে গ্যাসের মোল ভগ্নাংশ (দ্রাব্যতা), \(p\) হলো আংশিক বাষ্পচাপ এবং \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) হলো হেনরি ধ্রুবক।</p>
<p><strong>ধাপ ২: দ্রাব্যতা এবং হেনরি ধ্রুবকের সম্পর্ক</strong><br/>
নির্দিষ্ট চাপে গ্যাসের দ্রাব্যতা \(x\), হেনরি ধ্রুবক \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\)-এর ব্যস্তানুপাতিক। অর্থাৎ \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\)-এর মান যত বেশি হবে, গ্যাসটির দ্রাব্যতা তত কম হবে; এবং \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\)-এর মান যত কম হবে, দ্রাব্যতা তত বেশি হবে।</p>
<p><strong>ধাপ ৩: প্রদত্ত মানসমূহের তুলনা</strong><br/>
প্রদত্ত \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\)-এর মানগুলিকে অধঃক্রমে সাজালে পাই:
\[K_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathrm{Ar}) = 40.39 > K_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathrm{CO}_2) = 1.67 > K_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathrm{CH}_4) = 0.413 > K_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathrm{HCHO}) = 1.83 \times 10^{-5}\]
যেহেতু দ্রাব্যতা \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\)-এর ব্যস্তানুপাতিক, তাই দ্রাব্যতার ঊর্ধ্বক্রম হবে:
\[\mathrm{Ar} < \mathrm{CO}_2 < \mathrm{CH}_4 < \mathrm{HCHO}\]</p>
<p><strong>ধাপ ৪: চূড়ান্ত সিদ্ধান্ত</strong><br/>
অতএব, গ্যাসগুলির দ্রাব্যতার সঠিক ঊর্ধ্বক্রমটি হলো \(\mathrm{Ar} < \mathrm{CO}_2 < \mathrm{CH}_4 < \mathrm{HCHO}\)।</p>]]></text>

    <questiontext format="html">
      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Note :</strong> In the following questions two or more options may be correct.</p><p>Which of the following factor (s) affect the solubility of a gaseous solute in the fixed volume of liquid solvent?</p><p>(a) nature of solute<br/>(b) temperature<br/>(c) pressure</p>

<p><strong>দ্রষ্টব্য :</strong> নিচের প্রশ্নগুলিতে দুই বা ততোধিক বিকল্প সঠিক হতে পারে।</p><p>একটি নির্দিষ্ট আয়তনের তরল দ্রাবকে গ্যাসীয় দ্রাবের দ্রাব্যতাকে নিচের কোন বিষয় বা বিষয়গুলি প্রভাবিত করে?</p><p>(a) দ্রাবের প্রকৃতি<br/>(b) তাপমাত্রা<br/>(c) চাপ</p>]]></text>

    <generalfeedback format="html">
      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principles of Gas Solubility</strong><br/>
The solubility of a gaseous solute in a fixed volume of a liquid solvent depends fundamentally on three factors:<br/>
1. The chemical nature of the solute gas and the liquid solvent (polar gases such as ammonia and hydrogen chloride are much more soluble in water than nonpolar gases such as nitrogen and oxygen).<br/>
2. Temperature: The dissolution of gases in liquids is typically an exothermic process; hence, by Le Chatelier's principle, solubility decreases with increasing temperature.<br/>
3. Pressure: In accordance with Henry's law, the solubility of a gas is directly proportional to the applied partial pressure of the gas above the liquid.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Stated Conditions</strong><br/>
At constant temperature, the solubility is governed by the chemical nature of the solute and the pressure.<br/>
At constant pressure, the solubility is governed by the chemical nature of the solute and the temperature.<br/>
Therefore, both combinations correctly identify the interdependent factors affecting gas solubility under the respective specified conditions.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Solubility is influenced by the nature of the solute and pressure at constant temperature, and by the nature of the solute and temperature at constant pressure.</p>

      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle of Colligative Properties</strong><br/>
Colligative properties are those physical properties of solutions that depend strictly on the total number or concentration of solute particles present in a given amount of solvent, and are completely independent of the chemical identity, size, or nature of the solute particles.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Relative Lowering of Vapour Pressure</strong><br/>
For a solution of a non-volatile, non-electrolyte solute, Raoult's law gives:
\[\frac{p_1^\circ - p_1}{p_1^\circ} = x_2\]
Here, the relative lowering of vapour pressure depends solely on the mole fraction (concentration) of the non-electrolyte solute and not on its chemical nature.<br/>
For an electrolyte solute that dissociates into ions:
\[\frac{p_1^\circ - p_1}{p_1^\circ} = i \cdot x_2\]
Here, it depends directly on the total number of dissociated solute particles (ions) in the solution and does not depend on the specific chemical nature of those particles.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Relative lowering of vapour pressure is a colligative property because it depends on the concentration of a non-electrolyte solute without depending on its nature, and it depends on the number of particles of an electrolyte solute without depending on the nature of the solute particles.</p>

    <name><text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_SOLUTIONS_MCQ_Q30 - Expressions for Vant Hoff factor definition</text></name>
    <questiontext format="html">
      <text><![CDATA[<p>Van't Hoff factor \(i\) is given by the expression ________.</p>
<hr/>
<p>ভান্ট হফ গুণক \(i\)-এর প্রকাশক রাশিমালাটি হলো ________।</p>]]></text>
    </questiontext>
    <generalfeedback format="html">
      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle of the Van't Hoff Factor</strong><br/>
The van't Hoff factor \(i\) is introduced to account for the extent of association or dissociation of solute particles in solution. It corrects colligative property calculations when a solute does not behave as an ideal non-electrolyte.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Formulation in Terms of Colligative Properties and Molar Mass</strong><br/>
By definition, the van't Hoff factor is the ratio of the experimentally observed colligative property to the theoretical colligative property calculated assuming no association or dissociation:
\[i = \frac{\text{Observed colligative property}}{\text{Calculated colligative property}}\]
Because every colligative property is inversely proportional to the molar mass of the solute (\(\text{Colligative property} \propto \frac{1}{M}\)), the ratio of colligative properties inverts when expressed in terms of molar masses:
\[i = \frac{\text{Normal molar mass}}{\text{Abnormal molar mass}}\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
The correct expressions for the van't Hoff factor are the normal molar mass divided by the abnormal molar mass, and the observed colligative property divided by the calculated colligative property.</p>

    <generalfeedback format="html">
      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle of Isotonic Solutions</strong><br/>
Two solutions that exert the same osmotic pressure at a given temperature are defined as isotonic solutions:
\[\Pi_1 = \Pi_2\]
Using the van't Hoff equation for osmotic pressure (\(\Pi = iCRT\)), at the same temperature \(T\), equality of osmotic pressure requires that the total effective solute particle concentrations must be equal:
\[i_1 C_1 = i_2 C_2\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Colligative and Physical Properties</strong><br/>
Elevation in boiling point is given by \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{b}} = i K_{\mathrm{b}} m\), and depression in freezing point is given by \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} = i K_{\mathrm{f}} m\). Because both properties depend strictly on the effective concentration of solute particles, solutions in the same solvent with identical effective particle concentrations will exhibit identical elevation in boiling point and identical depression in freezing point.<br/>
In contrast, isotonic solutions can contain completely different solutes (for example, urea and glucose) and can have completely different solution densities.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Therefore, isotonic solutions must have the same elevation in boiling point and depression in freezing point.</p>

      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle of Azeotropic Mixtures</strong><br/>
Binary liquid mixtures that distill at a constant temperature without undergoing any change in composition between the liquid and vapour phases are called azeotropes. Non-ideal solutions showing large positive or negative deviations from Raoult's law form azeotropes at specific characteristic compositions.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of the Mixtures</strong><br/>
1. Water and nitric acid form a maximum boiling azeotrope (at approximately 68% nitric acid and 32% water by mass) because strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding leads to negative deviation from Raoult's law.<br/>
2. Water and ethanol form a minimum boiling azeotrope (at approximately 95.4% ethanol by volume) because weaker unlike interactions lead to positive deviation from Raoult's law.<br/>
3. Benzene-toluene and n-hexane-n-heptane mixtures form nearly ideal solutions because interactions between like and unlike molecules are essentially identical. Ideal solutions do not form azeotropes and can be completely separated by fractional distillation.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
The binary mixtures that have the same composition in the liquid and vapour phases are water-nitric acid and water-ethanol.</p>

    <name><text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_SOLUTIONS_MCQ_Q33 - Fundamental characteristics of isotonic solutions</text></name>
    <questiontext format="html">
      <text><![CDATA[<p>In isotonic solutions ________.</p>
<hr/>
<p>আইসোটোনিক দ্রবণসমূহের ক্ষেত্রে ________।</p>]]></text>

    <generalfeedback format="html">
      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Definition of Isotonic Solutions</strong><br/>
By scientific definition, isotonic solutions are two or more solutions that exert identical osmotic pressure at a given temperature (\(\Pi_1 = \Pi_2\)). When separated by a semipermeable membrane, no net osmotic flow of solvent occurs between them.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Solute and Solvent Composition</strong><br/>
Isotonicity requires only that the effective molar concentration of solute particles (\(iC\)) be equal at the specified temperature. It does not impose any restriction on the chemical identity of the solute or the solvent. For example, an aqueous solution of 0.1 M glucose is isotonic with an aqueous solution of 0.1 M urea, showing that solutes may differ while the solvent is the same. Solutions in different solvents can also exert the same osmotic pressure under appropriate conditions.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Therefore, in isotonic solutions, osmotic pressure is the same, and solute and solvent may or may not be the same.</p>

      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Governing Formula of Ideal Binary Solutions</strong><br/>
For a binary ideal liquid mixture of two volatile components 1 and 2, each component obeys Raoult's law across the entire range of composition:
\[p_1 = p_1^\circ x_1 \quad \text{and} \quad p_2 = p_2^\circ x_2\]
According to Dalton's law of partial pressures, the total vapour pressure \(p_{\mathrm{total}}\) is:
\[p_{\mathrm{total}} = p_1 + p_2 = p_1^\circ x_1 + p_2^\circ (1 - x_1) = p_2^\circ + (p_1^\circ - p_2^\circ) x_1\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Mathematical Nature of the Curve</strong><br/>
The expression for \(p_{\mathrm{total}}\) is a strictly linear equation in terms of mole fraction \(x_1\). Therefore, the plot of total vapour pressure versus mole fraction is a straight line.<br/>
Depending on whether component 1 is more volatile than component 2 (\(p_1^\circ > p_2^\circ\)) or less volatile (\(p_1^\circ < p_2^\circ\)), the straight line slopes upward or downward from one pure component vapour pressure to the other.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Curve Interpretation and Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Non-linear, bowed curves signify non-ideal behavior (positive or negative deviation from Raoult's law). Thus, the straight linear graphs represent the variation of total vapour pressure with composition for an ideal binary solution.</p>

      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Origin of Colligative Properties</strong><br/>
Colligative properties (relative lowering of vapour pressure, boiling point elevation, freezing point depression, and osmotic pressure) originate from the decrease in vapour pressure of a volatile solvent when a non-volatile solute is dissolved in it.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Solute and Solvent States</strong><br/>
When a non-volatile solute—whether in the solid state (such as sucrose, glucose, or urea) or in the liquid state (a liquid with negligible vapour pressure compared to the solvent)—is dissolved in a volatile liquid solvent, non-volatile solute particles occupy part of the liquid surface. This reduces the fraction of the surface occupied by volatile solvent molecules, decreasing the rate of evaporation and lowering the equilibrium vapour pressure of the solvent.<br/>
In contrast, dissolving a gas or another volatile liquid creates significant solute vapour pressure, so classical colligative lowering is not observed in that form.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Colligative properties are observed when a non-volatile solid is dissolved in a volatile liquid, and when a non-volatile liquid is dissolved in another volatile liquid.</p>

2. Henry's law: States that the solubility/partial pressure of a gas above a solution is directly proportional to its mole fraction in solution: \(p = K_{\mathrm{H}} \cdot x\).<br/>
3. Elevation of boiling point: The elevation in boiling point is directly proportional to the molality of the solute: \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{b}} = K_{\mathrm{b}} m\).<br/>
4. Depression in freezing point: The depression in freezing point is directly proportional to the molality of the solute: \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} = K_{\mathrm{f}} m\).<br/>
5. Osmotic pressure: According to the van't Hoff equation, osmotic pressure is directly proportional to molar concentration and temperature: \(\Pi = CRT\).</p>

      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Definitions and Mathematical Formulas for Concentration Units</strong><br/>
1. Mass percentage: Defined as the mass of the solute component per 100 g of solution:
\[\frac{\text{Mass of the solute component in solution}}{\text{Total mass of the solution}} \times 100\]
2. Volume percentage: Defined as the volume of the solute component per 100 mL of solution:
\[\frac{\text{Volume of the solute component in solution}}{\text{Total volume of solution}} \times 100\]
3. Mole fraction: Defined as the ratio of the number of moles of a given component to the total number of moles of all components present in the solution:
\[\frac{\text{Number of moles of a component}}{\text{Total number of moles of all the components}}\]
4. Molality: Defined as the number of moles of solute dissolved per kilogram (1000 g) of solvent:
\[\frac{\text{Number of moles of the solute components}}{\text{Mass of solvent in kilograms}}\]
5. Molarity: Defined as the number of moles of solute dissolved per litre of solution:
\[\frac{\text{Number of moles of the solute component}}{\text{Volume of solution in litres}}\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Mass percentage matches with the ratio of solute mass to total solution mass multiplied by 100; Volume percentage matches with the ratio of solute volume to total solution volume multiplied by 100; Mole fraction matches with moles of component divided by total moles; Molality matches with moles of solute per mass of solvent in kilograms; and Molarity matches with moles of solute per volume of solution in litres.</p>
