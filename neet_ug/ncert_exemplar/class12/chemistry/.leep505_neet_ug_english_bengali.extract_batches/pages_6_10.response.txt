<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Properties</strong><br/>Because micelle formation requires exceeding the Kraft temperature, it does not occur at all temperatures. Furthermore, micellisation is reversible: upon dilution of the soap solution below the CMC, the micelles break apart and revert to individual ions.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Therefore, micelle formation by soap in aqueous solution occurs above a particular concentration (CMC), and on dilution of the soap solution, micelles may revert to individual ions.</p>

  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Given Data and Conditions</strong><br/>The Freundlich adsorption isotherm is given by \(\frac{x}{m} = k p^{\frac{1}{n}}\), where \(x\) is the mass of the gas adsorbed on mass \(m\) of the adsorbent at pressure \(p\), and \(k\) and \(n\) are constants (\(n &gt; 1\) in general).</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Assumptions and Sign Conventions</strong><br/>We examine the limiting behaviour of \(\frac{x}{m}\) as a function of pressure \(p\) at constant temperature.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Relevant Formulae and Principles</strong><br/>\[\frac{x}{m} = k p^{\frac{1}{n}}\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Step-by-Step Working</strong><br/>When \(\frac{1}{n} = 0\), substituting into the isotherm gives:<br/>\[\frac{x}{m} = k p^{0} = k = \text{constant}\]<br/>Since \(\frac{x}{m}\) equals a constant \(k\), the extent of adsorption is independent of pressure \(p\). Plotting \(\frac{x}{m}\) along the \(y\)-axis against \(p\) along the \(x\)-axis yields a horizontal straight line parallel to the \(x\)-axis (corresponding to the constant-adsorption saturation limit).</p>
<p><strong>Step 5: Check and Final Answer</strong><br/>Therefore, when \(\frac{1}{n} = 0\), adsorption is independent of pressure, and the plot of \(\frac{x}{m}\) vs \(p\) in this constant-adsorption limit (denoted as \(n = 0\) in the source text) is a line parallel to the \(x\)-axis.</p>

<p><strong>ধাপ ২: অনুমান ও চিহ্ন প্রথা</strong><br/>স্থির তাপমাত্রায় চাপ \(p\)-এর সাপেক্ষে \(\frac{x}{m}\)-এর সীমাস্থ আচরণ বিশ্লেষণ করা হলো।</p>
<p><strong>ধাপ ৩: প্রাসঙ্গিক সূত্র ও নীতি</strong><br/>\[\frac{x}{m} = k p^{\frac{1}{n}}\]</p>

  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>\(\text{H}_2\) gas is adsorbed on activated charcoal to a very little extent in comparison to easily liquefiable gases due to ________.</p><hr/><p>সহজে তরলীকরণযোগ্য গ্যাসের তুলনায় সক্রিয় চারকোলের উপর \(\text{H}_2\) গ্যাস খুব সামান্য পরিমাণে অধিশোষিত হয় ________-এর কারণে।</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>Physisorption of a gas on a solid adsorbent depends on the nature of the gas, specifically its ease of liquefaction, which is directly related to its critical temperature and the strength of intermolecular van der Waals forces.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Application</strong><br/>Gases with higher critical temperatures have stronger van der Waals forces of attraction and are more easily liquefied, so they are adsorbed to a greater extent. Hydrogen (\(\text{H}_2\)) has a very low critical temperature (\(33\text{ K}\)) and very weak van der Waals interactions, making it difficult to liquefy and resulting in very little adsorption on activated charcoal.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Hence, \(\text{H}_2\) gas is adsorbed to a very little extent due to very weak van der Waals forces and a very low critical temperature.</p>
<hr/>

<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Therefore, reversible colloids and lyophilic colloids cannot be coagulated easily.</p>

The spontaneous passage of solvent molecules through a semipermeable membrane from pure solvent towards the solution side is osmosis.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Hence, movement of dispersion medium in an electric field is electroosmosis, solvent flow towards the solvent side is reverse osmosis, movement of charged colloidal particles towards electrodes is electrophoresis, and solvent flow towards the solution side is osmosis.</p>

  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>Colloids are characterised by their protective action, physical states of the dispersed phase and dispersion medium, and the sign of charge acquired by preferential adsorption of ions from solution.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Matching Each Item</strong><br/>1. Lyophilic colloids form a protective layer around lyophobic sols and hence act as protective colloids.<br/>2. A liquid-liquid colloidal system is called an emulsion.<br/>3. When \(\text{FeCl}_3\) is added to an excess of hot water, hydrated ferric oxide sol is formed, which preferentially adsorbs \(\text{Fe}^{3+}\) ions from the solution and becomes a positively charged colloid.<br/>4. When \(\text{FeCl}_3\) is treated with \(\text{NaOH}\), the resulting hydrated ferric oxide sol preferentially adsorbs \(\text{OH}^-\) ions from the alkaline medium and becomes a negatively charged colloid.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Therefore, protective colloid corresponds to lyophilic colloids, liquid-liquid colloid to emulsion, positively charged colloid to \(\text{FeCl}_3 + \text{hot water}\), and negatively charged colloid to \(\text{FeCl}_3 + \text{NaOH}\).</p>

  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>Colloidal systems are named according to the physical states of the dispersed phase and the dispersion medium.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Matching Each Item</strong><br/>1. A solid dispersed in a liquid is called a sol.<br/>2. A liquid dispersed in a solid is called a gel.<br/>3. A liquid dispersed in a liquid is called an emulsion.<br/>4. A gas dispersed in a liquid is called a foam.</p>
