  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>Which of the following options are correct?</p><hr/><p>निम्नलिखित में से कौन-से विकल्प सही हैं?</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>
Soap molecules in aqueous solution behave as normal strong electrolytes at low concentrations, dissociating into individual sodium or potassium cations and fatty acid anions. Above a specific temperature known as the Kraft temperature, and above a characteristic threshold concentration known as the critical micelle concentration, these surfactant ions aggregate to form associated colloids called micelles.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Concentration and Temperature Dependence</strong><br/>
Micelle formation cannot occur at all temperatures because it strictly requires temperatures above the Kraft temperature. It also does not happen at arbitrarily low concentrations; an adequate concentration exceeding the critical micelle concentration is required for spontaneous aggregation driven by hydrophobic interactions of the non-polar hydrocarbon tails.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Effect of Dilution and Electrolytic Behavior</strong><br/>
When a concentrated soap solution is diluted with water, the surfactant concentration drops below the critical micelle concentration. As a result, the aggregated colloidal micelles dissociate and revert back to individual ions. Consequently, soap solution does not behave as a normal strong electrolyte at all concentrations, but transitions into an associated colloidal system at higher concentrations.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Therefore, the correct assertions are that micelle formation by soap in aqueous solution occurs above a particular concentration, and on dilution of soap solution micelles may revert to individual ions.
</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>चरण 1: संकल्पनात्मक सिद्धांत</strong><br/>
जलीय विलयन में साबुन के अणु कम सांद्रता पर सामान्य प्रबल विद्युतअपघट्य की तरह व्यवहार करते हैं, जो सोडियम या पोटैशियम धनायनों और वसीय अम्ल ऋणायनों में वियोजित होते हैं। एक विशिष्ट तापमान, जिसे क्राफ्ट तापमान कहा जाता है, से ऊपर तथा एक विशिष्ट सीमांत सांद्रता, जिसे क्रांतिक मिसेल सांद्रता कहा जाता है, से ऊपर ये पृष्ठसक्रियक आयन पुंजित होकर संगुणित कोलॉइड बनाते हैं जिन्हें मिसेल कहते हैं।

  <name><text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_SURFACE_CHEMISTRY_MCQ_Q27 - Properties and actions of solid catalysts</text></name>
  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>Which of the following statements are correct about solid catalyst?</p><hr/><p>ठोस उत्प्रेरक के संदर्भ में निम्नलिखित में से कौन-से कथन सही हैं?</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>
A solid catalyst accelerates the rate of a chemical reaction by providing an alternative reaction pathway having a lower activation energy barrier. It alters the kinetics of the forward and backward reactions equally without shifting the chemical equilibrium position or altering the thermodynamic state functions.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Thermodynamic Invariance and Selectivity</strong><br/>
Because enthalpy of reaction, \(\Delta H\), depends solely on the initial and final thermodynamic states of the reactants and products, a catalyst does not alter \(\Delta H\) or the standard Gibbs free energy change of the reaction. Furthermore, solid catalysts possess characteristic selectivity, meaning that different catalysts can direct the reaction of identical starting reactants along different reaction pathways to yield completely different products.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Catalyst Quantity and Chemisorption Dependence</strong><br/>
Solid catalysts act through surface adsorption and are regenerated chemically unchanged at the end of the catalytic cycle; hence, only minute quantities are required rather than large amounts. Additionally, the catalytic activity of a solid catalyst crucially depends on the strength of chemisorption: reactant molecules must be chemisorbed reasonably strongly to be activated, but not so strongly that they become immobilized and block active sites from further reaction.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Therefore, the correct statements are that identical reactants may yield different products by using different catalysts, and a catalyst does not change the reaction enthalpy \(\Delta H\).
</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>चरण 1: संकल्पनात्मक सिद्धांत</strong><br/>
एक ठोस उत्प्रेरक कम संक्रियण ऊर्जा अवरोध वाला एक वैकल्पिक अभिक्रिया पथ प्रदान करके रासायनिक अभिक्रिया की दर को बढ़ाता है। यह रासायनिक साम्यावस्था की स्थिति को बदले बिना अथवा ऊष्मागतिकीय अवस्था फलनों को परिवर्तित किए बिना अग्र और प्रतीप दोनों अभिक्रियाओं की गतिकी को समान रूप से परिवर्तित करता है।
</p>
<p><strong>चरण 2: ऊष्मागतिकीय अपरिवर्तनीयता और वरणात्मकता</strong><br/>
चूँकि अभिक्रिया की एन्थैल्पी, \(\Delta H\), केवल अभिकारकों और उत्पादों की प्रारंभिक और अंतिम ऊष्मागतिकीय अवस्थाओं पर निर्भर करती है, इसलिए उत्प्रेरक \(\Delta H\) या मानक गिब्स मुक्त ऊर्जा परिवर्तन को परिवर्तित नहीं करता है। इसके अतिरिक्त, ठोस उत्प्रेरकों में विशिष्ट वरणात्मकता होती है, जिसका अर्थ है कि भिन्न-भिन्न उत्प्रेरक समान अभिकारकों की अभिक्रिया को विभिन्न मार्गों पर निर्देशित करके पूरी तरह से भिन्न उत्पाद दे सकते हैं।
</p>
<p><strong>चरण 3: उत्प्रेरक की मात्रा और रसोशोषण निर्भरता</strong><br/>
ठोस उत्प्रेरक पृष्ठीय अधिशोषण के माध्यम से कार्य करते हैं और उत्प्रेरकी चक्र के अंत में रासायनिक रूप से अपरिवर्तित पुनः प्राप्त हो जाते हैं; अतः बड़ी मात्रा के बजाय केवल सूक्ष्म मात्रा की आवश्यकता होती है। इसके अलावा, एक ठोस उत्प्रेरक की उत्प्रेरकी सक्रियता रसोशोषण की सामर्थ्य पर अत्यंत निर्भर करती है: अभिकारक अणुओं को सक्रिय होने के लिए पर्याप्त रूप से दृढ़ता से रसोशोषित होना चाहिए, परंतु इतना दृढ़ भी नहीं कि वे गतिहीन होकर आगे की अभिक्रिया के लिए सक्रिय केंद्रों को अवरुद्ध कर दें।
</p>
<p><strong>चरण 4: अंतिम निष्कर्ष</strong><br/>
अतः सही कथन यह हैं कि भिन्न उत्प्रेरकों का उपयोग करके समान अभिकारक भिन्न उत्पाद दे सकते हैं, तथा उत्प्रेरक अभिक्रिया की \(\Delta H\) को परिवर्तित नहीं करता है।

  <name><text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_SURFACE_CHEMISTRY_MCQ_Q28 - Analysis of Freundlich adsorption isotherm expression</text></name>
  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>Freundlich adsorption isotherm is given by the expression \(\frac{x}{m} = k p^{\frac{1}{n}}\). Which of the following conclusions can be drawn from this expression?</p><hr/><p>फ्रेंडलिक अधिशोषण समतापी को व्यंजक \(\frac{x}{m} = k p^{\frac{1}{n}}\) द्वारा व्यक्त किया जाता है। इस व्यंजक से निम्नलिखित में से कौन-से निष्कर्ष निकाले जा सकते हैं?</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Given Data and Conditions</strong><br/>
The Freundlich adsorption isotherm equation is given as \(\frac{x}{m} = k p^{1/n}\), where \(x/m\) represents the mass of gas adsorbed per unit mass of the adsorbent, \(p\) is the equilibrium gas pressure, and \(k\) and \(n\) are empirical constants depending on the nature of the adsorbent and gas at a fixed temperature (with \(n &gt; 1\)).
</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Case when the exponent reciprocal is zero</strong><br/>
When \(\frac{1}{n} = 0\), the expression simplifies to \(\frac{x}{m} = k p^0 = k\). This indicates that the extent of adsorption \(x/m\) becomes a constant value, entirely independent of the gas pressure \(p\).
</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Case when the parameter n approaches zero</strong><br/>
When \(\frac{1}{n} = 0\) (or formally in the saturation limit where \(1/n \to 0\)), the extent of adsorption does not vary with pressure. A plot of \(x/m\) versus \(p\) at high pressure becomes a horizontal straight line parallel to the pressure axis.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Check and Final Answer</strong><br/>
Thus, when \(\frac{1}{n} = 0\), the adsorption is independent of pressure, and when the exponent \(1/n = 0\), the plot of \(x/m\) versus \(p\) is a straight line parallel to the horizontal axis.
</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>चरण 1: दिए गए आँकड़े और शर्तें</strong><br/>
फ्रेंडलिक अधिशोषण समतापी समीकरण \(\frac{x}{m} = k p^{1/n}\) द्वारा दिया गया है, जहाँ \(x/m\) अधिशोषक के प्रति इकाई द्रव्यमान पर अधिशोषित गैस के द्रव्यमान को दर्शाता है, \(p\) साम्यावस्था गैस दाब है, तथा \(k\) और \(n\) एक निश्चित तापमान पर अधिशोषक और गैस की प्रकृति पर निर्भर करने वाले अनुभवजन्य स्थिरांक हैं (जहाँ \(n &gt; 1\))।
</p>
<p><strong>चरण 2: जब घातांक का व्युत्क्रम शून्य हो</strong><br/>
जब \(\frac{1}{n} = 0\) होता है, तो व्यंजक सरल होकर \(\frac{x}{m} = k p^0 = k\) हो जाता है। यह दर्शाता है कि अधिशोषण की मात्रा \(x/m\) एक स्थिर मान बन जाती है, जो गैस के दाब \(p\) से पूर्णतः स्वतंत्र होती है।
</p>
<p><strong>चरण 3: जब संतृप्ति सीमा में घातांक शून्य हो</strong><br/>
जब \(\frac{1}{n} = 0\) होता है (उच्च दाब पर संतृप्ति सीमा में), तो अधिशोषण की मात्रा दाब के साथ परिवर्तित नहीं होती है। उच्च दाब पर \(p\) के विरुद्ध \(x/m\) का आलेख दाब अक्ष के समांतर एक क्षैतिज सरल रेखा बन जाता है।
</p>
<p><strong>चरण 4: जाँच और अंतिम निष्कर्ष</strong><br/>
अतः जब \(\frac{1}{n} = 0\) होता है, तो अधिशोषण दाब से स्वतंत्र होता है, तथा \(p\) के विरुद्ध \(x/m\) का आलेख क्षैतिज अक्ष के समांतर एक रेखा होता है।

<question type="multichoice">
  <name><text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_SURFACE_CHEMISTRY_MCQ_Q29 - Low physisorption of hydrogen on charcoal</text></name>
  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>\(\text{H}_2\) gas is adsorbed on activated charcoal to a very little extent in comparison to easily liquefiable gases due to ________.</p><hr/><p>आसानी से द्रवीभूत होने वाली गैसों की तुलना में सक्रियित चारकोल पर \(\text{H}_2\) गैस बहुत कम मात्रा में अधिशोषित होती है, इसका कारण ________ है।</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>
Physical adsorption of gases on solid surfaces is governed by weak non-specific attractive intermolecular van der Waals forces. The extent of physisorption of a gas is directly correlated with the ease of liquefaction of the gas, which is quantified by its critical temperature \(T_c\).
</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Critical Temperature and Intermolecular Forces</strong><br/>
A higher critical temperature indicates stronger intermolecular attractive forces, making the gas more readily liquefiable and consequently more extensively adsorbed on activated charcoal. Conversely, dihydrogen has an extremely small molecular size and low polarizability, resulting in exceptionally weak dispersion forces.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Application to Dihydrogen Gas</strong><br/>
The critical temperature of dihydrogen gas is exceptionally low, approximately \(33\text{ K}\). Because of these very weak van der Waals forces and very low critical temperature, dihydrogen molecules exhibit very little tendency to condense or adhere to the charcoal surface, resulting in minimal physisorption compared to easily liquefiable gases like sulfur dioxide, ammonia, or carbon dioxide.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Therefore, the low extent of adsorption of dihydrogen on activated charcoal is attributed to very weak van der Waals forces and a very low critical temperature.
</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>चरण 1: संकल्पनात्मक सिद्धांत</strong><br/>
ठोस सतहों पर गैसों का भौतिक अधिशोषण दुर्बल अविशिष्ट आकर्षक अंतराण्विक वान डर वाल्स बलों द्वारा नियंत्रित होता है। किसी गैस के भौतिक अधिशोषण की मात्रा गैस के द्रवीकरण की सुगमता से सीधे सहसंबद्ध होती है, जिसे इसके क्रांतिक तापमान \(T_c\) द्वारा निर्धारित किया जाता है।
</p>

Mixing two oppositely charged sols in stoichiometric amounts neutralizes their surface charges, which eliminates electrostatic repulsive barriers and causes mutual coagulation rather than stabilization. Glucose and urea are covalent organic compounds that dissolve in water as uncharged neutral molecules. Because they do not dissociate into ions, they do not provide electrical counter-ions to neutralize surface charges or induce coagulation.

</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Stability Mechanisms</strong><br/>
Lyophilic colloids are inherently reversible systems; if separated by evaporation, the sol can be readily reconstituted simply by re-mixing with the dispersion medium. Their exceptional stability arises from two simultaneous factors: the presence of electrical surface charge and extensive protective solvation (hydration) envelopes surrounding each colloidal particle.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Comparison with Lyophobic Sols</strong><br/>
Lyophobic colloids (which are irreversible systems) owe their stability solely to surface electrical charges. The addition of small amounts of electrolytes rapidly neutralizes these charges and causes prompt coagulation. In contrast, coagulating lyophilic or reversible colloids requires not only neutralizing the surface charge with an electrolyte but also dehydrating the solvated sheath using a dehydrating agent like alcohol or acetone.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Therefore, reversible colloids and lyophilic colloids cannot be coagulated easily.
</p>

<p><strong>Step 2: Formation of Protective Layer</strong><br/>
When a lyophilic sol is added to a lyophobic sol, the lyophilic particles adsorb onto the surface of the lyophobic particles, forming a protective hydrophilic sheath or film around them.

<p><strong>Step 3: Protection Mechanism</strong><br/>
As a result of this surrounding film, the lyophobic particles acquire the surface characteristics of lyophilic particles. Consequently, the lyophobic particles are shielded from direct contact with coagulating electrolyte ions, preventing aggregation. The lyophilic colloid acting in this manner is designated as a protective colloid.
</p>

  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle of Electrokinetic Phenomena</strong><br/>
Colloidal dispersions exhibit electrokinetic phenomena because of the presence of an electrical double layer at the interface between the charged dispersed phase particles and the oppositely charged dispersion medium.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Electrophoresis versus Electroosmosis</strong><br/>
Under the influence of an applied electric field, if colloidal particles are free to migrate toward the oppositely charged electrode, the process is known as electrophoresis. However, if the movement of the colloidal particles is mechanically prevented (for instance, by retaining them behind a suitable semipermeable membrane or porous plug), the mobile dispersion medium itself begins to move in the electric field toward the electrode corresponding to its charge sign.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Definition and Direction of Movement</strong><br/>
This electrokinetic phenomenon in which the dispersion medium moves under the influence of an applied electric potential while the colloidal particles are immobilized is termed electroosmosis.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Therefore, when electrophoresis is prevented, electroosmosis takes place and the dispersion medium begins to move.
</p>

</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Chemical and Quantitative Constancy</strong><br/>
Because the catalyst does not enter the stoichiometric balance of products and undergoes regeneration at the end of the catalytic cycle, its chemical composition and its total mass (quantity) remain strictly unchanged throughout the overall reaction.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Physical and Qualitative Alterations</strong><br/>
During the course of the reaction, a solid catalyst frequently undergoes physical modifications. Its surface texture, particle size, crystal morphology, or physical state may change (for example, smooth manganese dioxide crystals become finely divided powder after catalysing the thermal decomposition of potassium chlorate). </p>

  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle of Adsorption versus Absorption</strong><br/>
Adsorption is a surface phenomenon involving the accumulation of molecular species at the surface rather than in the bulk of a solid or liquid. In contrast, absorption is a bulk phenomenon where the substance is uniformly distributed throughout the interior of the solid or liquid phase.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Observation on Dipping Chalk in Ink</strong><br/>
Ink is a colloidal dispersion containing colored pigment/dye particles suspended or dissolved in a liquid solvent (usually water). When a stick of chalk is dipped in ink, the chalk retains color only on its outer surface. If the stick is snapped open, the interior of the chalk remains white but is visibly moist.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Mechanism of Separation</strong><br/>
The larger colored dye molecules are selectively retained at the chalk surface through physical adsorption. Meanwhile, the smaller solvent molecules penetrate deeper through capillary action into the porous core of the chalk through absorption.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Therefore, the phenomena occurring are adsorption of the colored substance and absorption of the solvent.
</p>

<p><strong>Step 2: Passing Sulphur Vapour Through Cold Water</strong><br/>

Egg white consists primarily of albumin, which is a naturally occurring polymeric protein whose individual molecules are sufficiently large to fall directly into colloidal dimensions. When whipped with water, it forms a macromolecular colloid.

  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principles of Transport Phenomena</strong><br/>
Transport processes across interfaces and membranes in solutions and colloidal dispersions can be driven by osmotic pressure differences or applied electric fields.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Electrokinetic Transport Phenomena</strong><br/>
When an external electric potential is applied across a colloidal dispersion, the charged colloidal particles migrate toward the oppositely charged electrode; this directional motion is termed electrophoresis. If the particles are held stationary, the mobile dispersion medium itself moves under the influence of the electric field, which is termed electroosmosis.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Membrane Transport Phenomena</strong><br/>
When a solution and pure solvent are separated by a semipermeable membrane, solvent molecules spontaneously flow through the membrane from the dilute side into the solution side; this process is termed osmosis. When an external hydrostatic pressure greater than the osmotic pressure is applied to the solution side, the net flow of solvent molecules is reversed, forcing solvent from the solution side toward the pure solvent side; this phenomenon is termed reverse osmosis.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Final Match Synthesis</strong><br/>
Movement of the dispersion medium in an electric field corresponds to electroosmosis; passage of solvent molecules through a semipermeable membrane toward the solvent side corresponds to reverse osmosis; migration of charged colloidal particles toward oppositely charged electrodes under an applied potential corresponds to electrophoresis; and passage of solvent molecules through semipermeable membranes toward the solution side corresponds to osmosis.
</p>

  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>Match the items given in Column I and Column II.</p><hr/><p>कॉलम I और कॉलम II में दी गई मदों को सुमेलित कीजिए।</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principles of Colloidal Systems</strong><br/>
Colloidal dispersions are classified by solvent affinity, physical phase combinations, and the preferential adsorption of ions leading to characteristic surface electrical charges.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Protective Colloids and Liquid-Liquid Sols</strong><br/>
Lyophilic colloids possess extensive solvation layers and have the property of forming protective films around lyophobic particles, shielding them from coagulation by electrolytes; hence, lyophilic colloids serve as protective colloids. Liquid-liquid colloidal dispersions, in which both the dispersed phase and the dispersion medium are liquids, are called emulsions.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Charge Development on Hydrated Ferric Oxide Sols</strong><br/>
When ferric chloride solution is added to an excess of boiling water, hydrolysis produces hydrated ferric oxide particles that preferentially adsorb common ferric cations (\(\text{Fe}^{3+}\)), creating a positively charged colloidal sol: \(\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3\cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}/\text{Fe}^{3+}\). Conversely, when ferric chloride is added to an excess of sodium hydroxide solution, the precipitate preferentially adsorbs hydroxide anions (\(\text{OH}^-\)), yielding a negatively charged colloidal sol: \(\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3\cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}/\text{OH}^-\).
</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Final Match Synthesis</strong><br/>
Protective colloid corresponds to lyophilic colloids; liquid-liquid colloid corresponds to emulsion; positively charged colloid corresponds to \(\text{FeCl}_3 + \text{hot water}\); and negatively charged colloid corresponds to \(\text{FeCl}_3 + \mathrm{NaOH}\).
</p>

<p><strong>चरण 1: कोलॉइडी निकायों के संकल्पनात्मक सिद्धांत</strong><br/>

</p>
<p><strong>चरण 2: रक्षी कोलॉइड और द्रव-द्रव सॉल</strong><br/>
द्रवरागी कोलॉइड व्यापक विलायकन परतें रखते हैं और द्रवविरागी कणों के चारों ओर सुरक्षात्मक परत बनाने का गुण रखते हैं, जो उन्हें विद्युतअपघट्य द्वारा स्कंदन से बचाती है; अतः द्रवरागी कोलॉइड रक्षी कोलॉइड के रूप में कार्य करते हैं। द्रव-द्रव कोलॉइडी परिक्षेपण, जिसमें परिक्षिप्त प्रावस्था और परिक्षेपण माध्यम दोनों द्रव होते हैं, पायस कहलाते हैं।

Colloidal processes encompass methods used for preparing sols, purifying colloidal solutions, utilizing surface phenomena in daily applications, and precipitating charged dispersed phases.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Dialysis and Peptisation</strong><br/>
Dialysis is a purification process based on the differential diffusion rates through an animal or parchment membrane, which allows crystalloid ions to pass through while retaining colloidal particles, thereby purifying the sol. Peptisation is a method of converting a freshly prepared precipitate back into a stable colloidal sol by shaking it with a dispersion medium in the presence of a small amount of an electrolyte (peptising agent).
</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Emulsification and Electrophoresis</strong><br/>
Emulsification is the stabilizing of droplets of one liquid in another, which is the exact mechanism underlying the cleansing action of soap: the hydrophobic tail dissolves oil or grease while the polar head remains in water, emulsifying grease droplets into water for removal. Electrophoresis involves the migration of charged colloidal particles under an external electric field; when the particles reach the oppositely charged electrode, they lose their charge and coagulate, making it a technique related to coagulation.
</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Final Match Synthesis</strong><br/>
Dialysis corresponds to purification; peptisation corresponds to colloidal sol formation; emulsification corresponds to cleansing action of soap; and electrophoresis corresponds to coagulation.
