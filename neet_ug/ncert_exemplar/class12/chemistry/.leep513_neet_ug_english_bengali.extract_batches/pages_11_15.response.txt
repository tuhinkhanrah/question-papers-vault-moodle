Nucleophilic substitution of alkyl halides with excess ammonia yields primary amines alongside secondary, tertiary amines and quaternary ammonium salts. Hence, ammonolysis corresponds to the reaction of alkyl halides with \(\mathrm{NH}_3\).<br/>Gabriel phthalimide synthesis: This synthetic method for preparing primary aliphatic amines involves treating phthalimide with ethanolic \(\mathrm{KOH}\) to generate potassium phthalimide, which then undergoes nucleophilic substitution with an alkyl halide (\(\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{X}\)) to yield N-alkylphthalimide, followed by alkaline hydrolysis. Hence, it corresponds to the reaction of phthalimide with \(\mathrm{KOH}\) and \(\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{X}\).<br/>Hoffmann bromamide reaction: When an acid amide is treated with bromine and aqueous or ethanolic sodium hydroxide, degradation of the amide occurs with the loss of the carbonyl carbon as carbonate, yielding a primary amine having one carbon atom less than the starting amide. Hence, it corresponds to the formation of an amine with a lesser number of carbon atoms.<br/>Carbylamine reaction: Both aliphatic and aromatic primary amines on warming with chloroform and alcoholic \(\mathrm{KOH}\) produce intensely offensive-smelling isocyanides (carbylamines). This reaction is used specifically as a diagnostic detection test for primary amines. Hence, it corresponds to the detection test for primary amines.</p><p><strong>Final Conclusion</strong><br/>The correct pairings are: Ammonolysis matches reaction of alkyl halides with \(\mathrm{NH}_3\); Gabriel phthalimide synthesis matches reaction of phthalimide with \(\mathrm{KOH}\) and \(\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{X}\); Hoffmann bromamide reaction matches amine with lesser number of carbon atoms; and Carbylamine reaction matches detection test for primary amines.</p>

  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>Different nitrogen-containing compounds and derivatives exhibit characteristic chemical structures, reagents, and functional reactivity.</p><p><strong>Evaluation of Matches</strong><br/>Benzene sulphonyl chloride: Benzenesulphonyl chloride (\(\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_5\mathrm{SO}_2\mathrm{Cl}\)) is known as Hinsberg's reagent. It is utilized to distinguish between primary, secondary, and tertiary amines based on the solubility of the resulting sulphonamides in alkali. Hence, it corresponds to Hinsberg reagent.<br/>Sulphanilic acid: Sulphanilic acid contains both a basic amino group (\(-\mathrm{NH}_2\)) and an acidic sulphonic acid group (\(-\mathrm{SO}_3\mathrm{H}\)) on the same benzene ring. An intramolecular proton transfer from the acidic group to the basic amino group produces an internal dipolar salt structure called a Zwitter ion (\(\mathrm{H}_3\overset{+}{\mathrm{N}}-\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_4-\mathrm{SO}_3^-\)). Hence, it corresponds to Zwitter ion.<br/>Alkyl diazonium salts: Aliphatic primary amines react with nitrous acid to form alkyl diazonium salts (\(\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{N}_2^+\mathrm{X}^-\)), which are extremely unstable even at \(0^\circ\mathrm{C}\) and spontaneously lose nitrogen gas in aqueous solution, converting quantitatively into carbocations that react with water to form alcohols. Hence, it corresponds to conversion to alcohols.<br/>Aryl diazonium salts: Arenediazonium salts (\(\mathrm{Ar}-\mathrm{N}_2^+\mathrm{X}^-\)) are relatively stable in cold aqueous solution (\(0\text{--}5^\circ\mathrm{C}\)) and undergo azo coupling reactions with electron-rich aromatic compounds (such as phenols and aromatic amines) to produce brilliantly coloured extended conjugated azo dyes. Hence, it corresponds to dyes.</p><p><strong>Final Conclusion</strong><br/>The correct pairings are: Benzene sulphonyl chloride matches Hinsberg reagent; Sulphanilic acid matches Zwitter ion; Alkyl diazonium salts match conversion to alcohols; and Aryl diazonium salts match dyes.</p>
