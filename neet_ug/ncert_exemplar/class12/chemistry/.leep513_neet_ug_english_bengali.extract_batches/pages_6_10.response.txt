  <name><text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_AMINES_MCQ_Q24 - Strongest Bronsted base among cyclic and acyclic amines</text></name>
  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>Among the following amines, the strongest Brönsted base is __________.</p>
<hr/>
<p>নিচের অ্যামিনগুলির মধ্যে সবচেয়ে শক্তিশালী ব্রনস্টেড ক্ষারক কোনটি?</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>A Brönsted base functions as a proton acceptor via an unshared pair of electrons on its nitrogen atom. The basic strength depends primarily on the availability of this electron pair to coordinate with a proton and the stability of the conjugate acid formed upon protonation.</p><p><strong>Evaluation of the Amines</strong><br/>In aniline, the lone pair of electrons on the nitrogen atom is conjugated with the aromatic \( \pi \)-electron system and is delocalised into the benzene ring through resonance (+R effect). This significantly decreases the electron density on nitrogen and suppresses its proton-accepting ability.<br/>In ammonia (\(\mathrm{NH}_3\)), the nitrogen atom is \(\mathrm{sp}^3\) hybridized and its lone pair is localized without resonance delocalization, but it lacks alkyl groups to provide electron-donating inductive (+I) stabilization to the conjugate acid.<br/>In pyrrole, the unshared electron pair on nitrogen contributes directly to the aromatic sextet (\(6\pi\) electrons) of the five-membered ring. Protonation of pyrrole at nitrogen destroys the aromatic resonance stability, making pyrrole an exceptionally weak base.<br/>In pyrrolidine, a saturated secondary heterocyclic amine, the nitrogen atom is \(\mathrm{sp}^3\) hybridized and its lone pair is completely localized. The two adjacent electron-releasing methylene groups exert an inductive (+I) effect that increases electron density on the nitrogen atom and stabilizes the conjugate ammonium cation without any disruption of aromaticity. As a result, pyrrolidine is a much stronger base than ammonia, aniline, and pyrrole.</p><p><strong>Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Pyrrolidine is the strongest Brönsted base among the given amines.</p>

<p><strong>ধারণাগত নীতি</strong><br/>ব্রনস্টেড ক্ষারক নাইট্রোজেন পরমাণুর নিঃসঙ্গ ইলেকট্রন জোড়ের মাধ্যমে প্রোটন গ্রহীতা হিসেবে কাজ করে। ক্ষারকীয় তীব্রতা মূলত প্রোটন গ্রহণের জন্য নাইট্রোজেনের ইলেকট্রন জোড়ের সহজলভ্যতা এবং প্রোটন গ্রহণের পর উৎপন্ন অনুবন্ধী অ্যাসিডের স্থায়িত্বের উপর নির্ভর করে।</p><p><strong>অ্যামিনসমূহের মূল্যায়ন</strong><br/>অ্যানিলিনে নাইট্রোজেন পরমাণুর নিঃসঙ্গ ইলেকট্রন জোড়টি রেজোন্যান্সের (+R প্রভাব) মাধ্যমে বেনজিন বলয়ে স্থানান্তরিত হয়। ফলে নাইট্রোজেনের উপর ইলেকট্রন ঘনত্ব হ্রাস পায় এবং এর প্রোটন গ্রহণের প্রবণতা কমে যায়।<br/>অ্যামোনিয়ায় (\(\mathrm{NH}_3\)) নাইট্রোজেন পরমাণু \(\mathrm{sp}^3\) সংকরিত এবং এর নিঃসঙ্গ জোড়টি রেজোন্যান্সমুক্ত হলেও এতে অ্যালকাইল গ্রুপের মতো ইলেকট্রন দানকারী আবেশীয় (+I) প্রভাব অনুপস্থিত থাকে।<br/>পাইরোলে নাইট্রোজেনের নিঃসঙ্গ ইলেকট্রন জোড়টি পঞ্চসদস্যবিশিষ্ট বলয়ের অ্যারোমেটিক ষটচক্রের (\(6\pi\) ইলেকট্রন) একটি অপরিহার্য অংশ। পাইরোলের নাইট্রোজেনে প্রোটন যুক্ত হলে বলয়ের অ্যারোমেটিক স্থায়িত্ব নষ্ট হয়ে যায়, তাই পাইরোল অত্যন্ত দুর্বল ক্ষারক।<br/>পাইরোলিডিন একটি সম্পৃক্ত গৌণ বিষমচাক্রিক অ্যামিন, যার নাইট্রোজেন পরমাণুটি \(\mathrm{sp}^3\) সংকরিত এবং এর নিঃসঙ্গ ইলেকট্রন জোড় সম্পূর্ণ স্থানীয়কৃত। বলয়ের দুটি পার্শ্ববর্তী অ্যালকাইল অংশ কার্যকর আবেশীয় (+I) প্রভাবের মাধ্যমে নাইট্রোজেনের ইলেকট্রন ঘনত্ব বৃদ্ধি করে এবং অ্যারোমেটিসিটি বিনষ্ট না করেই অনুবন্ধী অ্যামোনিয়াম ক্যাটায়নকে স্থিতিশীল করে। ফলস্বরূপ, প্রদত্ত অ্যামিনগুলির মধ্যে পাইরোলিডিনের ক্ষারকত্ব সর্বোচ্চ।</p><p><strong>চূড়ান্ত সিদ্ধান্ত</strong><br/>প্রদত্ত অ্যামিনগুলির মধ্যে পাইরোলিডিন হলো সবচেয়ে শক্তিশালী ব্রনস্টেড ক্ষারক।</p>]]></text></generalfeedback>

  <name><text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_AMINES_MCQ_Q25 - Decreasing order of basic strength of oxygen and nitrogen species</text></name>
  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>The correct decreasing order of basic strength of the following species is __________.<br/>\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}, \mathrm{NH}_3, \mathrm{OH}^-, \mathrm{NH}_2^-\)</p>
<hr/>
<p>নিচের প্রজাতিগুলির ক্ষারকীয় তীব্রতার সঠিক অধঃক্রম কোনটি?<br/>\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}, \mathrm{NH}_3, \mathrm{OH}^-, \mathrm{NH}_2^-\)</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>Basic strength is governed by the ability of a species to donate a pair of electrons to a proton. Negatively charged conjugate bases are always stronger bases than their corresponding neutral parent molecules. Furthermore, for atoms in the same period of the periodic table, basicity is inversely proportional to electronegativity, because less electronegative atoms hold their valence electrons less tightly and donate them more readily.</p><p><strong>Evaluation of Anionic and Neutral Species</strong><br/>Between the anions \(\mathrm{NH}_2^-\) and \(\mathrm{OH}^-\), both carry a full negative charge. Nitrogen is less electronegative (\(\approx 3.0\)) than oxygen (\(\approx 3.5\)). Consequently, nitrogen accommodates the negative charge less stably and donates its electron pair to a proton far more aggressively than oxygen does. Thus, the amide ion (\(\mathrm{NH}_2^-\)) is a stronger base than the hydroxide ion (\(\mathrm{OH}^-\)).<br/>Similarly, between the neutral molecules \(\mathrm{NH}_3\) and \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\), the lower electronegativity of nitrogen compared to oxygen means that ammonia donates its lone pair of electrons to a proton much more readily than water does. Thus, ammonia (\(\mathrm{NH}_3\)) is a stronger base than water (\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\)).<br/>Because anionic species are intrinsically stronger bases than neutral species from comparable atoms, the overall sequence of basic strength follows: \(\mathrm{NH}_2^- > \mathrm{OH}^- > \mathrm{NH}_3 > \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\).</p><p><strong>Final Conclusion</strong><br/>The correct decreasing order of basic strength is \(\mathrm{NH}_2^- > \mathrm{OH}^- > \mathrm{NH}_3 > \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\).</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>ধারণাগত নীতি</strong><br/>ক্ষারকীয় তীব্রতা প্রোটনকে ইলেকট্রন জোড় প্রদানের ক্ষমতার উপর নির্ভর করে। ঋণাত্মক আধানযুক্ত অনুবন্ধী ক্ষারকসমূহ সর্বদা তাদের সংশ্লিষ্ট প্রশম অণুর তুলনায় অনেক শক্তিশালী ক্ষারক হয়। তদুপরি, পর্যায় সারণির একই পর্যায়ের মৌলসমূহের ক্ষেত্রে ক্ষারকত্ব তড়িৎঋণাত্মকতার ব্যস্তানুপাতিক, কারণ কম তড়িৎঋণাত্মক পরমাণু তার যোজ্যতা ইলেকট্রনকে কম দৃঢ়ভাবে ধরে রাখে এবং সহজে দান করতে পারে।</p><p><strong>অ্যানায়ন ও প্রশম প্রজাতির মূল্যায়ন</strong><br/>ঋণাত্মক অ্যানায়ন \(\mathrm{NH}_2^-\) এবং \(\mathrm{OH}^-\)-এর মধ্যে, নাইট্রোজেনের তড়িৎঋণাত্মকতা (\(\approx 3.0\)) অক্সিজেনের (\(\approx 3.5\)) তুলনায় কম। ফলে নাইট্রোজেন ঋণাত্মক আধানকে অক্সিজেনের মতো দৃঢ়ভাবে ধরে রাখতে পারে না এবং অধিকতর সহজে প্রোটনকে ইলেকট্রন দান করে। তাই অ্যামাইড আয়ন (\(\mathrm{NH}_2^-\)) হাইড্রোক্সাইড আয়নের (\(\mathrm{OH}^-\)) চেয়ে শক্তিশালী ক্ষারক।<br/>একইভাবে প্রশম অণু \(\mathrm{NH}_3\) এবং \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\)-এর ক্ষেত্রে, নাইট্রোজেনের কম তড়িৎঋণাত্মকতার কারণে অ্যামোনিয়ার নিঃসঙ্গ জোড় পানির অক্সিজেন অপেক্ষা সহজে প্রোটন গ্রহণ করে। ফলে অ্যামোনিয়া (\(\mathrm{NH}_3\)) পানির (\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\)) চেয়ে শক্তিশালী ক্ষারক।<br/>অ্যানায়নসমূহ সংশ্লিষ্ট প্রশম প্রজাতির চেয়ে তীব্র ক্ষারক হওয়ায় সামগ্রিক ক্ষারকত্বের অধঃক্রম হলো: \(\mathrm{NH}_2^- > \mathrm{OH}^- > \mathrm{NH}_3 > \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\)।</p><p><strong>চূড়ান্ত সিদ্ধান্ত</strong><br/>ক্ষারকীয় তীব্রতার সঠিক অধঃক্রম হলো \(\mathrm{NH}_2^- > \mathrm{OH}^- > \mathrm{NH}_3 > \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\)।</p>]]></text></generalfeedback>

  <name><text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_AMINES_MCQ_Q26 - Identification of most volatile organic compound</text></name>
  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>Which of the following should be most volatile?</p><p>(I) \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{NH}_2\)<br/>(II) \((\mathrm{CH}_3)_3\mathrm{N}\)<br/>(III) \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{NHCH}_3\)<br/>(IV) \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{CH}_3\)</p>
<hr/>
<p>নিচের যৌগগুলির মধ্যে কোনটি সর্বাধিক উদ্বায়ী হবে?</p><p>(I) \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{NH}_2\)<br/>(II) \((\mathrm{CH}_3)_3\mathrm{N}\)<br/>(III) \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{NHCH}_3\)<br/>(IV) \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{CH}_3\)</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>Volatility is inversely related to boiling point, which depends directly on the strength of intermolecular attractive forces. Compounds with weaker intermolecular forces require less thermal energy to vaporize and are therefore more volatile.</p><p><strong>Evaluation of Intermolecular Forces</strong><br/>Primary amine \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{NH}_2\) has two hydrogen atoms attached to nitrogen and forms extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonds, leading to a relatively high boiling point (\(\approx 48^\circ\mathrm{C}\)).<br/>Secondary amine \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{NHCH}_3\) has one hydrogen atom attached to nitrogen and also exhibits intermolecular hydrogen bonding, giving a boiling point of \(\approx 36^\circ\mathrm{C}\).<br/>Tertiary amine \((\mathrm{CH}_3)_3\mathrm{N}\) has no hydrogen atom attached to nitrogen and cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds with itself. However, it is a polar molecule possessing permanent dipole-dipole attractions, resulting in a boiling point of \(\approx 3^\circ\mathrm{C}\).<br/>Propane (\(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{CH}_3\)) is a nonpolar hydrocarbon having only weak London dispersion forces between molecules. As London dispersion forces are the weakest among all these intermolecular forces, propane boils at \(-42^\circ\mathrm{C}\). With the lowest boiling point, propane is by far the most volatile compound among the four.</p><p><strong>Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Propane (\(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{CH}_3\), represented as IV) is the most volatile.</p>

Subsequent alkaline or acidic hydrolysis of N-alkylphthalimide cleaves the phthalyl moiety to generate phthalic acid salt and releases the primary aliphatic amine (\(\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{NH}_2\)). The carbon chain attached to the amino group in \(\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{NH}_2\) originates solely from the alkyl halide precursor (\(\mathrm{R}-\mathrm{X}\)) and thus retains the exact same number of carbon atoms in the chain.<br/>By comparison, treatment of an amide with bromine in aqueous sodium hydroxide (Hoffmann bromamide degradation) eliminates the carbonyl carbon as carbonate, yielding an amine with one less carbon atom than the starting amide.<br/>Reduction of nitrites with \(\mathrm{LiAlH}_4\) produces alcohols and ammonia, rather than amines.<br/>Hence, heating an alkyl halide with potassium phthalimide followed by hydrolysis cleanly delivers a primary amine with the identical carbon chain length as the alkyl halide reactant.</p><p><strong>Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Heating alkylhalide with potassium salt of phthalimide followed by hydrolysis yields an amine having the same number of carbon atoms in the chain as in the alkyl halide reactant.</p>

  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>Which of the following cannot be prepared by Sandmeyer's reaction?</p>
<hr/>

  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>Sandmeyer's reaction is a synthetic method in which a diazonium group (\(-\mathrm{N}_2^+\mathrm{X}^-\)) attached to an aromatic ring is replaced by a halogen atom (chlorine or bromine) using cuprous halides (\(\mathrm{Cu}_2\mathrm{Cl}_2/\mathrm{HCl}\) or \(\mathrm{Cu}_2\mathrm{Br}_2/\mathrm{HBr}\)) as catalysts.</p><p><strong>Evaluation of Halobenzene Preparations</strong><br/>Chlorobenzene is prepared via Sandmeyer's reaction by treating benzenediazonium chloride with cuprous chloride dissolved in concentrated hydrochloric acid (\(\mathrm{Cu}_2\mathrm{Cl}_2/\mathrm{HCl}\)).<br/>Bromobenzene is likewise prepared via Sandmeyer's reaction using cuprous bromide dissolved in hydrobromic acid (\(\mathrm{Cu}_2\mathrm{Br}_2/\mathrm{HBr}\)).<br/>Iodobenzene does not require a cuprous catalyst; it is prepared simply by warming or shaking the aqueous benzenediazonium chloride solution with potassium iodide (\(\mathrm{KI}\)).   <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>The reagents that can be used to convert benzenediazonium chloride to benzene are __________.</p>

On warming with benzenediazonium chloride, ethanol reduces the diazonium salt to benzene and is itself oxidized to acetaldehyde (ethanal):<br/>\[\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_5\mathrm{N}_2^+\mathrm{Cl}^- + \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{OH} \longrightarrow \mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_6 + \mathrm{N}_2 + \mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CHO} + \mathrm{HCl}\]<br/>In contrast, reduction with stannous chloride and hydrochloric acid (\(\mathrm{SnCl}_2/\mathrm{HCl}\)) reduces the diazonium salt to phenylhydrazine (\(\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_5\mathrm{NHNH}_2\)), rather than benzene.<br/>Lithium aluminium hydride (\(\mathrm{LiAlH}_4\)) is a powerful nucleophilic reducing agent that reacts violently with diazonium salts, causing decomposition and multiple side reactions without providing benzene cleanly.<br/>Therefore, ethanol (\(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{OH}\)) and hypophosphorous acid (\(\mathrm{H}_3\mathrm{PO}_2\)) are the effective reagents for converting benzenediazonium chloride to benzene.</p><p><strong>Final Conclusion</strong><br/>\(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{OH}\) and \(\mathrm{H}_3\mathrm{PO}_2\) convert benzenediazonium chloride to benzene.</p>

  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>Arenium ion involved in the bromination of aniline is __________.</p>
<hr/>
<p>অ্যানিলিনের ব্রোমিনেশনে জড়িত অ্যারিনিয়াম আয়ন হলো __________।</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>Electrophilic aromatic substitution proceeds via the formation of a resonance-stabilised carbocation intermediate known as an arenium ion (or Wheland intermediate / \(\sigma\)-complex). In the case of aniline, the strongly electron-donating amino group (\(-\mathrm{NH}_2\)) delocalises the positive charge over the ortho and para positions of the ring as well as onto the exocyclic nitrogen atom.</p><p><strong>Evaluation of Arenium Ion Resonance Structures</strong><br/>When the electrophile (\(\mathrm{Br}^+\)) attacks the ortho position, the resulting arenium ion is stabilised by several resonance forms: the carbocation charge resides on the ring carbons adjacent to the site of attack, and crucially, the nitrogen lone pair can form a double bond to the ring, yielding a particularly stable quinoid structure with a complete octet on every atom and a formal positive charge on nitrogen (\(=\!\mathrm{NH}_2^+\)). Both the ring carbocation form and the quinoid iminium form represent authentic resonance contributors of the arenium intermediate for ortho-attack.<br/>Similarly, when the electrophile attacks the para position, the positive charge is delocalised through the ring to the carbon bearing the amino group and directly onto the nitrogen atom, generating an equivalent quinoid contributor (\(=\!\mathrm{NH}_2^+\)).<br/>In contrast, a structure where the positive charge is improperly depicted on a ring carbon without maintaining correct conjugation or valid valency does not represent a contributing form of the arenium ion.<br/>Thus, the resonance contributors corresponding to ortho-attack and para-attack with proper charge delocalisation onto nitrogen and ring positions represent the true arenium ion intermediates involved in the reaction.</p><p><strong>Final Conclusion</strong><br/>The resonance structures representing proper delocalisation of positive charge onto the nitrogen and the activated positions of the ring are valid arenium ions involved in the bromination of aniline.</p>

This is a standard and correct nucleophilic substitution reaction.<br/>Reaction of tert-butyl chloride with aqueous potassium hydroxide: aqueous \(\mathrm{KOH}\) is predominantly a nucleophile rather than a strong non-nucleophilic base; it undergoes nucleophilic substitution (\(\mathrm{S_N1}\)) to yield tert-butyl alcohol (\((\mathrm{CH}_3)_3\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{OH}\)) as the major product, rather than elimination to 2-methylpropene (which requires alcoholic \(\mathrm{KOH}\)). Thus, predicting 2-methylpropene as the major product with aqueous \(\mathrm{KOH}\) is incorrect.<br/>Reaction of chlorocyclohexane with alcoholic potassium hydroxide: alcoholic \(\mathrm{KOH}\) provides the strongly basic ethoxide ion in ethanol, which promotes dehydrohalogenation via an \(\mathrm{E2}\) elimination mechanism, cleanly producing cyclohexene. This reaction is correct.<br/>Reaction of propylamine with nitrous acid: propylamine is a three-carbon primary amine (\(\mathrm{C}_3\mathrm{H}_7\mathrm{NH}_2\)). Reaction with \(\mathrm{HNO}_2\) at \(0^\circ\mathrm{C}\) forms an aliphatic diazonium intermediate that decomposes immediately with loss of \(\mathrm{N}_2\) to yield a mixture of propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol, and propene. It cannot yield tert-butyl alcohol, which has four carbons. Thus, this reaction is incorrect.<br/>Therefore, the first and third reactions are chemically correct.</p><p><strong>Final Conclusion</strong><br/>The reaction of ethyl chloride with ammonia to form ethylamine and the dehydrohalogenation of chlorocyclohexane with alcoholic potassium hydroxide to form cyclohexene are correct.</p>

  <name><text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_AMINES_MCQ_Q37 - Identification of electrophilic aromatic substitution reactions</text></name>
  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>Which of the following reactions belong to electrophilic aromatic substitution?</p>
<hr/>
<p>নিচের কোন বিক্রিয়াগুলি ইলেকট্রোফিলিক অ্যারোমেটিক প্রতিস্থাপনের অন্তর্ভুক্ত?</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>An electrophilic aromatic substitution (\(\mathrm{S_EAr}\)) reaction involves the replacement of a substituent (typically a hydrogen atom) attached to an aromatic ring by an incoming electrophile, proceeding through a resonance-stabilised arenium ion intermediate.</p><p><strong>Evaluation of the Reactions</strong><br/>Bromination of acetanilide: bromine in acetic acid generates the electrophile \(\mathrm{Br}^+\), which attacks the activated aromatic ring of acetanilide, replacing a hydrogen atom at the ortho or para position. Because substitution takes place directly on the aromatic ring by an electrophile, this is an electrophilic aromatic substitution reaction.<br/>Coupling reaction of aryldiazonium salts: the arenediazonium ion (\(\mathrm{ArN}_2^+\)) functions as a weak electrophile and attacks an activated aromatic ring (such as phenol or aniline), replacing a hydrogen atom to form an azo compound (\(\mathrm{Ar}-\mathrm{N}=\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{Ar}'\)). This reaction is an electrophilic aromatic substitution.<br/>Diazotisation of aniline: primary aromatic amine reacts with nitrous acid (\(\mathrm{NaNO}_2 + \mathrm{HCl}\)) at \(0\text{--}5^\circ\mathrm{C}\) where the nitrosonium ion (\(\mathrm{NO}^+\)) attacks the unshared electron pair of the amino nitrogen atom, forming a diazonium salt. This is a nucleophilic attack by nitrogen, not substitution on the aromatic ring.<br/>Acylation of aniline: acetyl chloride or acetic anhydride undergoes nucleophilic acyl substitution where the nitrogen atom of aniline acts as a nucleophile and attacks the carbonyl carbon, producing acetanilide. Substitution occurs at nitrogen, not on the benzene ring.<br/>Hence, bromination of acetanilide and azo coupling of aryldiazonium salts represent electrophilic aromatic substitution reactions.</p><p><strong>Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Bromination of acetanilide and coupling reaction of aryldiazonium salts are electrophilic aromatic substitution reactions.</p>
