    <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>Histamine stimulates the secretion of pepsin and hydrochloric acid in the stomach, and also causes allergic reactions by binding to different receptors in the body.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation</strong><br/>Brompheniramine and terfenadine (seldane) are antihistamines that act as antiallergic drugs by interfering with the natural action of histamine at allergy-related receptor sites; they do not affect stomach acid secretion.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Application</strong><br/>Ranitidine (Zantac) and cimetidine (Tegamet) are antihistamines designed specifically to prevent the interaction of histamine with the receptors present in the stomach wall, resulting in the release of lesser amounts of acid.</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Therefore, ranitidine and cimetidine are antihistamines that function as antacids.</p>
