By the fundamental rule of oxidation numbers, the algebraic sum of the oxidation numbers of all constituent atoms in an uncharged (neutral) molecule is strictly zero (\(0\)).<br/>3. A monoatomic hydrogen ion carrying a single unit positive charge (\(\mathrm{H}^+\)) has an oxidation number equal to its charge, which is \(+1\).<br/>4.   <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Evaluation of Assertion</strong><br/>In potassium permanganate (\(\mathrm{KMnO}_4\)), manganese is in its highest oxidation state of \(+7\). When reacted with potassium iodide (\(\mathrm{KI}\)), permanganate readily oxidises iodide ions (\(\mathrm{I}^-\)) to iodine (\(\mathrm{I}_2\)) in acidic medium or iodate (\(\mathrm{IO}_3^-\)) in alkaline/neutral medium, while itself getting reduced. The claim that the oxidation state of manganese increases from \(+2\) to \(+7\) describes an oxidation process, which is the exact opposite of what occurs.
