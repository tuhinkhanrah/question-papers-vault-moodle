Two atomic orbitals combine to form two molecular orbitals called bonding molecular orbital (BMO) and anti bonding molecular orbital (ABMO). Energies of various molecular orbitals for elements hydrogen to nitrogen increase in the order: \(\sigma 1s < \sigma^* 1s < \sigma 2s < \sigma^* 2s < (\pi 2p_x \simeq \pi 2p_y) < \sigma 2p_z < (\pi^* 2p_x \simeq \pi^* 2p_y) < \sigma^* 2p_z\) and for oxygen and fluorine order of energy of molecular orbitals is given below: \(\sigma 1s < \sigma^* 1s < \sigma 2s < \sigma^* 2s < \sigma 2p_z < (\pi 2p_x \simeq \pi 2p_y) < (\pi^* 2p_x \simeq \pi^* 2p_y) < \sigma^* 2p_z\). Different atomic orbitals of one atom combine with those atomic orbitals of the second atom which have comparable energies and proper orientation. Further, if the overlapping is head on, the molecular orbital is called &lsquo;Sigma&rsquo;, (\(\sigma\)) and if the overlap is lateral, the molecular orbital is called &lsquo;pi&rsquo;, (\(\pi\)). The molecular orbitals are filled with electrons according to the same rules as followed for filling of atomic orbitals. However, the order for filling is not the same for all molecules or their ions. Bond order is one of the most important parameters to compare the strength of bonds.</p><p>Which of the following statements is correct?</p>]]></text></questiontext>

When two oxygen atoms combine to form a dioxygen molecule (\(\mathrm{O}_2\)), these \(5 + 5 = 10\) atomic orbitals combine linearly to form exactly 10 molecular orbitals: five bonding molecular orbitals (\(\sigma 1s\), \(\sigma 2s\), \(\sigma 2p_z\), \(\pi 2p_x\), \(\pi 2p_y\)) and five antibonding molecular orbitals (\(\sigma^* 1s\), \(\sigma^* 2s\), \(\sigma^* 2p_z\), \(\pi^* 2p_x\), \(\pi^* 2p_y\)). Therefore, exactly 10 molecular orbitals are formed.</p><p><strong>Step 3: Evaluation of Orbital Occupancy in Dioxygen</strong><br/>Dioxygen has 16 electrons. Its ground-state molecular orbital configuration is:<br/>\[\sigma 1s^2 \, \sigma^* 1s^2 \, \sigma 2s^2 \, \sigma^* 2s^2 \, \sigma 2p_z^2 \, (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2) \, (\pi^* 2p_x^1 = \pi^* 2p_y^1)\]<br/>The antibonding orbitals \(\pi^* 2p_x\) and \(\pi^* 2p_y\) are singly occupied (half-filled), while \(\sigma^* 2p_z\) remains completely unoccupied; hence, not all molecular orbitals in dioxygen are completely filled.<br/>The total number of bonding molecular orbitals (5) is equal to the total number of antibonding molecular orbitals (5).<br/>There are five completely filled bonding molecular orbitals and only two completely filled antibonding molecular orbitals; hence, the number of filled bonding orbitals does not equal the number of filled antibonding orbitals.</p><p><strong>Step 4: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>The accurate description is that in the formation of dioxygen from oxygen atoms 10 molecular orbitals will be formed.</p>]]></text></generalfeedback>

  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>A nodal plane is a planar region where the probability density of finding an electron is zero. It has 2 nodal planes: one is the nodal plane containing the internuclear axis (\(xz\)-plane), and the second is perpendicular to the internuclear axis midway between the nuclei.<br/>For \(\sigma^* 2p_z\): Formed by the out-of-phase head-on combination of two \(2p_z\) orbitals directed along the \(z\)-axis. Each parent \(2p_z\) atomic orbital inherently contains a nodal plane passing through its respective nucleus (perpendicular to the \(z\)-axis). In addition, destructive out-of-phase combination introduces an additional nodal plane perpendicular to the internuclear axis midway between the two nuclei. Consequently, \(\sigma^* 2p_z\) possesses a total of 3 nodal planes.</p><p><strong>Step 3: Comparison and Final Conclusion</strong><br/>With 3 nodal planes, \(\sigma^* 2p_z\) contains the maximum number of nodal planes among the given molecular orbitals.</p>]]></text></generalfeedback>

  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>In homonuclear diatomic molecules, the relative energetic ordering of the \(\sigma 2p_z\) and degenerate \((\pi 2p_x, \pi 2p_y)\) molecular orbitals depends on the extent of \(2s-2p\) orbital mixing.</p><p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Mixing in Light Versus Heavy Diatomic Molecules</strong><br/>For lighter elements from boron to nitrogen (total electrons up to 14, such as \(\mathrm{B}_2\), \(\mathrm{C}_2\), and \(\mathrm{N}_2\)), the energy difference between the \(2s\) and \(2p\) atomic orbitals is relatively small.
