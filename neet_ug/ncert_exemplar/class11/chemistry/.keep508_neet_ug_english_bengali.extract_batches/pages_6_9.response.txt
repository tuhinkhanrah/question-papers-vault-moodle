    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Assertion (A):</strong> Among halogens fluorine is the best oxidant.</p><p><strong>Reason (R):</strong> Fluorine is the most electronegative atom.</p><hr/><p><strong>দৃঢ়োক্তি (A):</strong> হ্যালোজেনগুলির মধ্যে ফ্লুরিন হলো সর্বোত্তম জারক পদার্থ।</p><p><strong>কারণ (R):</strong> ফ্লুরিন হলো সর্বাধিক তড়িৎঋণাত্মক পরমাণু।</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Evaluation of Assertion</strong><br/>Fluorine has the highest standard reduction potential among all halogens (\(\mathrm{E}^{\ominus}_{\mathrm{F}_2/2\mathrm{F}^{-}} = +2.87\ \mathrm{V}\)), meaning that molecular fluorine has the greatest thermodynamic tendency to gain electrons and undergo reduction. Therefore, elemental fluorine is indeed the strongest oxidising agent (best oxidant) among the halogens, making the statement of assertion true.</p><p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Reason</strong><br/>Fluorine has an electronegativity value of \(4.0\) on the Pauling scale, which is the highest of any element in the periodic table. Thus, the statement of reason is also true.</p><p><strong>Step 3: Evaluation of Explanation</strong><br/>The oxidising power of a halogen in aqueous solution does not depend solely on electronegativity. Instead, it is governed by the standard reduction potential, which involves a thermodynamic cycle comprising the bond dissociation enthalpy of the halogen molecule (\(\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\text{diss}}H\)), the electron gain enthalpy of the gaseous halogen atom (\(\Delta_{\text{eg}}H\)), and the hydration enthalpy of the resulting halide ion (\(\Delta_{\text{hyd}}H\)). Fluorine is the best oxidant primarily due to its unusually low bond dissociation energy of the \(\mathrm{F}-\mathrm{F}\) single bond and the extraordinarily large negative hydration enthalpy of the small \(\mathrm{F}^{-}\) ion, rather than its electronegativity alone.
