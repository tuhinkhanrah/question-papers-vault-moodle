<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation</strong><br/>The decomposition of hydrogen peroxide is represented by:<br/>\[2\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2(\mathrm{aq}) \longrightarrow 2\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}(l) + \mathrm{O}_2(\mathrm{g})\]<br/>- In \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\), oxygen is in the \(-1\) oxidation state.<br/>- In \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\), oxygen is reduced to the \(-2\) oxidation state.<br/>- In \(\mathrm{O}_2\), oxygen is oxidised to the \(0\) oxidation state.<br/>Because the same element (oxygen) undergoes both oxidation and reduction simultaneously, this decomposition is a classic disproportionation reaction.
