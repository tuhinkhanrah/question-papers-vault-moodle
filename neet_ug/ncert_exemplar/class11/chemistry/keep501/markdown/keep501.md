$$

But we have only 0.19 mol HCl (aq), hence, HCl (aq) is limiting reagent.

So amount of $\mathrm{CaCl}_2$ formed will depend on the amount of HCl available.

Since, 2 mol HCl (aq) forms 1 mol of $\mathrm{CaCl}_2$, therefore, 0.19 mol of HCl (aq) would give:

$$
0.19\ \mathrm{mol}\ \mathrm{HCl}\ (\mathrm{aq}) \times \frac{1\ \mathrm{mol}\ \mathrm{CaCl}_2\ (\mathrm{aq})}{2\ \mathrm{mol}\ \mathrm{HCl}\ (\mathrm{aq})} = 0.095\ \mathrm{mol}
