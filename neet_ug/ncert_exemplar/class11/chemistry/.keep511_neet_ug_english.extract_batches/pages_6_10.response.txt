  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Evaluation of Assertion</strong><br/>In the three-dimensional network structure of silicon dioxide (\(\mathrm{SiO}_2\)), each silicon atom is tetrahedrally bonded to four oxygen atoms, where silicon carries a formal valence requirement of 4. When a trivalent aluminium atom replaces a tetravalent silicon atom, it supplies only three valence electrons instead of four. To satisfy the four-coordinate tetrahedral framework, an extra electron is required, which imparts a net negative charge to the aluminosilicate network. Therefore, the assertion is correct.</p><p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Reason</strong><br/>Aluminium belongs to Group 13 and is trivalent, having three valence electrons, whereas silicon belongs to Group 14 and is tetravalent, possessing four valence electrons. Therefore, the reason is correct.</p><p><strong>Step 3: Explanation Relationship</strong><br/>Because aluminium has one fewer valence electron than silicon (it is trivalent rather than tetravalent), its isomorphous substitution into the network creates an electron deficiency of one electron per substituted atom, directly resulting in a net negative charge that must be balanced by external cations. Thus, the reason correctly explains the assertion.</p><p><strong>Step 4: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct, and Reason (R) is the correct explanation of Assertion (A).</p>]]></text>

  <name><text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_MCQ_Q40 - Water repellent nature and structure of silicones</text></name>
  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Assertion (A):</strong> Silicons are water repelling in nature.</p>
<p><strong>Reason (R) :</strong> Silicons are organosilicon polymers, which have \(\left(-\mathrm{R}_2\mathrm{SiO}-\right)\) as repeating unit.</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Evaluation of Assertion</strong><br/>Silicones are water-repellent (hydrophobic) materials widely used in waterproofing textiles and sealants. Therefore, the assertion is correct.</p><p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Reason</strong><br/>Silicones are synthetic organosilicon polymers that contain repeating siloxane units of the general formula \(\left(-\mathrm{R}_2\mathrm{SiO}-\right)\), where \(\mathrm{R}\) represents an organic alkyl or aryl group. Therefore, the reason is correct.</p><p><strong>Step 3: Explanation Relationship</strong><br/>The specific reason why silicones are water-repellent is that the non-polar, hydrophobic organic alkyl or aryl groups (\(\mathrm{R}\)) face outward and form an oily, non-polar shield around the siloxane \(\mathrm{Si}-\mathrm{O}-\mathrm{Si}\) backbone, preventing interaction with water molecules. Merely stating that silicones are organosilicon polymers with \(\left(-\mathrm{R}_2\mathrm{SiO}-\right)\) repeating units classifies their structure but does not itself explain the cause of water repellency (which arises specifically from the hydrophobic surface presentation of the hydrocarbon groups). Thus, Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).</p><p><strong>Step 4: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are correct, but Reason (R) is not the correct explanation of Assertion (A).</p>]]></text>
