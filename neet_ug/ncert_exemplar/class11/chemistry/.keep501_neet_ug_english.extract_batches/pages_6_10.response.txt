  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p>Match the following:</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>
The mole is related to mass, molar volume of gas at STP, and Avogadro's number of molecules through the expressions:<br/>
\[n = \frac{m}{M} = \frac{V\text{ (at STP)}}{22.4\text{ L}} = \frac{N}{N_{\mathrm{A}}}\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Step-by-Step Evaluation of Each Item</strong><br/>
1. For \(88\text{ g}\) of \(\mathrm{CO}_2\):<br/>
Molar mass of \(\mathrm{CO}_2 = 12 + 2(16) = 44\text{ g mol}^{-1}\).<br/>
Amount in moles = \(\frac{88\text{ g}}{44\text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 2\text{ mol}\).<br/>
2. For \(6.022 \times 10^{23}\) molecules of \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\):<br/>
One mole of any substance contains \(6.022 \times 10^{23}\) entities, so this corresponds to \(1\text{ mol}\).<br/>
3. For \(5.6\text{ litres of }\mathrm{O}_2\text{ at STP}\):<br/>
At STP, \(1\text{ mol}\) occupies \(22.4\text{ L}\).<br/>
Amount in moles = \(\frac{5.6\text{ L}}{22.4\text{ L mol}^{-1}} = 0.25\text{ mol}\).<br/>
4. For \(96\text{ g}\) of \(\mathrm{O}_2\):<br/>
Molar mass of \(\mathrm{O}_2 = 32\text{ g mol}^{-1}\).<br/>
Amount in moles = \(\frac{96\text{ g}}{32\text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 3\text{ mol}\).<br/>
5. For \(1\text{ mol of any gas}\):<br/>
By definition of the mole, one mole contains \(6.022 \times 10^{23}\) molecules.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
The correct correspondences are:<br/>
- \(88\text{ g}\) of \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) corresponds to \(2\text{ mol}\).<br/>
- \(6.022 \times 10^{23}\) molecules of \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\) corresponds to \(1\text{ mol}\).<br/>
- \(5.6\text{ litres of }\mathrm{O}_2\text{ at STP}\) corresponds to \(0.25\text{ mol}\).<br/>
- \(96\text{ g}\) of \(\mathrm{O}_2\) corresponds to \(3\text{ mol}\).<br/>
- \(1\text{ mol of any gas}\) corresponds to \(6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ molecules}\).</p>]]></text>

Each physical quantity is defined in terms of base or derived SI/standard units according to international dimensional conventions.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Step-by-Step Evaluation of Each Quantity</strong><br/>
1. Molarity is the number of moles of solute per litre of solution; its unit is \(\text{mol L}^{-1}\).<br/>
2. Mole fraction is the ratio of moles of a component to total moles; being a ratio of identical units, it is Unitless.<br/>
3. Mole is the SI base unit for the amount of substance; its unit is \(\text{mol}\).<br/>
4. Molality is the number of moles of solute per kilogram of solvent; its unit is \(\text{mol kg}^{-1}\).<br/>
5. Pressure is force per unit area; in the SI system, its unit is the Pascal (\(\text{Pa}\)).<br/>
6. Luminous intensity is an SI base quantity; its base unit is the Candela (\(\text{cd}\)).<br/>
7. Density is mass per unit volume; a common laboratory unit is \(\text{g mL}^{-1}\).<br/>
8. Mass is an SI base quantity; its base unit is the kilogram (\(\text{kg}\)).</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
The correct matches are:<br/>
- Molarity corresponds to \(\text{mol L}^{-1}\).<br/>
- Mole fraction corresponds to \(\text{Unitless}\).<br/>
- Mole corresponds to \(\text{mol}\).<br/>
- Molality corresponds to \(\text{mol kg}^{-1}\).<br/>
- Pressure corresponds to \(\text{Pascal}\).<br/>
- Luminous intensity corresponds to \(\text{Candela}\).<br/>
- Density corresponds to \(\text{g mL}^{-1}\).<br/>
- Mass corresponds to \(\text{kg}\).</p>]]></text>

  <name><text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_SOMEBASICCONCEPTSOFCHEMISTRY_MCQ_Q38 - Empirical and molecular mass of ethene</text></name>
  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Assertion (A):</strong> The empirical mass of ethene is half of its molecular mass.</p>
<p><strong>Reason (R):</strong> The empirical formula represents the simplest whole number ratio of various atoms present in a compound.</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>
The empirical formula represents the simplest whole-number ratio of the different atoms present in a molecule of a compound, whereas the molecular formula shows the exact number of different atoms present in a molecule. The molecular formula is related to the empirical formula by an integer factor \(n\):<br/>
\[\text{Molecular formula} = (\text{Empirical formula})_n\]<br/>
\[\text{Molecular mass} = n \times (\text{Empirical formula mass})\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Assertion and Reason</strong><br/>
For ethene (\(\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{H}_4\)):<br/>
- Molecular mass of \(\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{H}_4 = (2 \times 12) + (4 \times 1) = 28\text{ g mol}^{-1}\).<br/>
- The simplest ratio of carbon to hydrogen atoms is \(2 : 4 = 1 : 2\), giving the empirical formula \(\mathrm{CH}_2\).<br/>
- Empirical formula mass of \(\mathrm{CH}_2 = 12 + (2 \times 1) = 14\text{ g mol}^{-1}\).<br/>
Here, \(n = \frac{28}{14} = 2\), which means the empirical mass is exactly half of the molecular mass. Hence, the Assertion is true.<br/>
The Reason correctly defines the empirical formula as the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms in a compound, and it directly explains why the empirical formula of ethene is \(\mathrm{CH}_2\) with an empirical mass that is half of the molecular mass (\(\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{H}_4\)). Hence, the Reason is true and correctly explains the Assertion.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.</p>]]></text>

  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Assertion (A):</strong> One atomic mass unit is defined as one twelfth of the mass of one carbon-12 atom.</p>
<p><strong>Reason (R):</strong> Carbon-12 isotope is the most abundant isotope of carbon and has been chosen as standard.</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>
One atomic mass unit (\(1\text{ amu}\) or \(1\text{ u}\)) is standardly defined as a mass exactly equal to one-twelfth of the mass of one \({}^{12}\mathrm{C}\) atom.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Assertion and Reason</strong><br/>
- Assertion: By international standard (IUPAC, 1961), \(1\text{ amu}\) is defined as one-twelfth of the mass of a single atom of carbon-12. Thus, the Assertion is true.<br/>
- Reason: Carbon-12 is indeed the most abundant natural isotope of carbon (abundance \(\approx 98.9\%\)) and has been chosen as the standard for defining atomic and molecular masses. Thus, the Reason is also a true statement.<br/>
- However, the definition of one atomic mass unit as one-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom is a matter of agreed international convention (chosen so that hydrogen has an atomic mass close to 1); the high natural abundance of carbon-12 does not provide the logical explanation for why the fraction \(\frac{1}{12}\) is selected in the definition. Hence, the Reason is not the correct explanation of the Assertion.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.</p>]]></text>

<question type="multichoice">
  <name><text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_SOMEBASICCONCEPTSOFCHEMISTRY_MCQ_Q40 - Significant figures in decimal measurement numbers</text></name>
  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Assertion (A):</strong> Significant figures for \(0.200\) is \(3\) where as for \(200\) it is \(1\).</p>
<p><strong>Reason (R):</strong> Zero at the end or right of a number are significant provided they are not on the right side of the decimal point.</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>
Rules for determining significant figures specify:<br/>
1. All non-zero digits are significant.<br/>
2. Zeros to the left of the first non-zero digit are not significant.<br/>
3. Zeros at the end or to the right of a number are significant only if they are on the right side of the decimal point.<br/>
4. Terminal zeros in an integer without a decimal point are not significant unless indicated by scientific notation.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Assertion and Reason</strong><br/>
- In \(0.200\), the zeros appear after a non-zero digit and to the right of a decimal point; hence both terminal zeros are significant, giving a total of \(3\) significant figures.<br/>
- In \(200\), there is no decimal point; hence the trailing zeros are not significant, giving only \(1\) significant figure. Thus, the Assertion is true.<br/>
- The Reason states that zeros at the end or right of a number are significant provided they are not on the right side of the decimal point. According to the standard rules of significant figures, trailing zeros are significant provided they ARE on the right side of a decimal point. Therefore, the Reason statement is false.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
A is true but R is false.</p>]]></text>

<question type="multichoice">
  <name><text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_SOMEBASICCONCEPTSOFCHEMISTRY_MCQ_Q41 - Stoichiometry of water in methane combustion</text></name>
  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Assertion (A):</strong> Combustion of \(16\text{ g}\) of methane gives \(18\text{ g}\) of water.</p>
<p><strong>Reason (R):</strong> In the combustion of methane, water is one of the products.</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Given Data and Conditions</strong><br/>
Mass of methane (\(\mathrm{CH}_4\)) = \(16\text{ g}\).<br/>
Combustion reaction: \(\mathrm{CH}_4(\mathrm{g}) + 2\mathrm{O}_2(\mathrm{g}) \longrightarrow \mathrm{CO}_2(\mathrm{g}) + 2\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{l})\).<br/>
Molar mass of \(\mathrm{CH}_4 = 12 + 4(1) = 16\text{ g mol}^{-1}\).<br/>
Molar mass of \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} = 2(1) + 16 = 18\text{ g mol}^{-1}\).</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Assumptions and Principles</strong><br/>
Combustion is assumed to be complete in excess oxygen, following stoichiometric mass relationships.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Relevant Formulae and Principles</strong><br/>
\[n(\mathrm{CH}_4) = \frac{m}{M} = \frac{16\text{ g}}{16\text{ g mol}^{-1}} = 1\text{ mol}\]<br/>
From the balanced equation, \(1\text{ mol}\) of \(\mathrm{CH}_4\) produces \(2\text{ mol}\) of \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\).<br/>
\[m(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}) = n \times M = 2\text{ mol} \times 18\text{ g mol}^{-1} = 36\text{ g}\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Step-by-Step Working and Evaluation</strong><br/>
- Complete combustion of \(16\text{ g}\) of methane yields \(36\text{ g}\) of water, not \(18\text{ g}\). Therefore, the Assertion is false.<br/>
- In the combustion reaction, carbon dioxide and water are the reaction products; hence water is indeed one of the products. Therefore, the Reason is true.</p>
<p><strong>Step 5: Check and Final Answer</strong><br/>
Since the Assertion is false and the Reason is true, the correct evaluation is that A is false but R is true.</p>]]></text>
