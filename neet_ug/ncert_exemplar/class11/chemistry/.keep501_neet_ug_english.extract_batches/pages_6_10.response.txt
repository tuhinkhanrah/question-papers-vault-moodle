  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>38. <strong>Assertion (A)</strong> : The empirical mass of ethene is half of its molecular mass.</p>
<p><strong>Reason (R)</strong> : The empirical formula represents the simplest whole number ratio of various atoms present in a compound.</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>The molecular formula gives the exact number of different types of atoms present in a molecule, whereas the empirical formula represents the simplest whole-number ratio of those atoms.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation</strong><br/>For ethene, the molecular formula is \(\mathrm{C_2H_4}\), giving a molecular mass of \(2(12) + 4(1) = 28\text{ u}\). Dividing the subscripts by 2 gives the simplest whole-number ratio, which is the empirical formula \(\mathrm{CH_2}\). Its empirical mass is \(12 + 2(1) = 14\text{ u}\), which is exactly half of \(28\text{ u}\). Therefore, the Assertion is true.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Application</strong><br/>The Reason accurately defines the empirical formula as representing the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms in a compound, and this simplification by a factor of 2 directly explains why the empirical mass of ethene is half its molecular mass.</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.</p>]]></text></generalfeedback>

  <name><text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_AR_SOMEBASICCONCEPTSOFCHEMISTRY_AR_Q40 - Significant figures for trailing zeros in numbers</text></name>
  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>40. <strong>Assertion (A)</strong> : Significant figures for 0.200 is 3 where as for 200 it is 1.</p>
<p><strong>Reason (R)</strong> : Zero at the end or right of a number are significant provided they are not on the right side of the decimal point.</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>According to the rules for determining significant figures, trailing zeros (zeros at the end or right of a number) are significant if and only if they are on the right side of the decimal point.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation</strong><br/>In \(0.200\), the leading zero is not significant, while the two trailing zeros to the right of the decimal point are significant; hence, \(0.200\) has 3 significant figures. In \(200\) (written without a decimal point), the trailing zeros are not significant; hence, \(200\) has 1 significant figure. Therefore, the Assertion is true.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Application</strong><br/>The Reason states the opposite of the rule—it claims trailing zeros are significant provided they are *not* on the right side of the decimal point. Thus, the Reason is false.</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>A is true but R is false.</p>]]></text></generalfeedback>

  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>41. <strong>Assertion (A)</strong> : Combustion of 16 g of methane gives 18 g of water.</p>
<p><strong>Reason (R)</strong> : In the combustion of methane, water is one of the products.</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>Complete combustion of a hydrocarbon such as methane (\(\mathrm{CH_4}\)) in oxygen produces carbon dioxide and water according to a balanced stoichiometric equation.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation</strong><br/>The balanced equation for the combustion of methane is:<br/>\[\mathrm{CH_4(g) + 2O_2(g) \longrightarrow CO_2(g) + 2H_2O(g)}\]<br/>\(16\text{ g}\) of \(\mathrm{CH_4}\) corresponds to \(1\text{ mol}\) of \(\mathrm{CH_4}\). From stoichiometry, \(1\text{ mol}\) of \(\mathrm{CH_4}\) produces \(2\text{ mol}\) of \(\mathrm{H_2O}\), which has a mass of \(2 \times 18\text{ g} = 36\text{ g}\), not \(18\text{ g}\). Therefore, the Assertion is false.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Application</strong><br/>The Reason states that water is one of the products in the combustion of methane, which is true.</p>
