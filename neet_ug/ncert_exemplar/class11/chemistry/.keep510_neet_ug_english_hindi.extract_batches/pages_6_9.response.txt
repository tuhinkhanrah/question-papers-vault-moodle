<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Each Element</strong><br/>1. Lithium (\(\mathrm{Li}\)) has the smallest ionic radius among alkali metals, resulting in an exceptionally high hydration enthalpy. This compensates for its high ionisation enthalpy and gives it the most negative standard electrode potential (\(E^\ominus = -3.04\text{ V}\)) among alkali metals.<br/>2. Sodium (\(\mathrm{Na}\)) forms sodium hydroxide (\(\mathrm{NaOH}\)), which acts as a very strong monoacidic base (matched here with the strongest monoacidic base property among the listed choices).<br/>3. Barium (\(\mathrm{Ba}\), atomic number 56) belongs to Group 2 and Period 6, so its outer electronic configuration is \(6s^2\). Additionally, barium sulphate (\(\mathrm{BaSO_4}\)) is insoluble in water because the hydration enthalpy of the large \(\mathrm{Ba}^{2+}\) ion is insufficient to overcome the lattice enthalpy of \(\mathrm{BaSO_4}\).</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Therefore, \(\mathrm{Li}\) corresponds to the most negative \(E^\ominus\) value among alkali metals, \(\mathrm{Na}\) corresponds to the strongest monoacidic base, \(\mathrm{Ca}\) corresponds to an insoluble oxalate, and \(\mathrm{Ba}\) corresponds to both an insoluble sulphate and a \(6s^2\) outer electronic configuration.</p>

  <name><text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_2025_SEC5_THESBLOCKELEMENTS_ASSERTIONREASON_Q44 - Thermal decomposition of lithium carbonate</text></name>
  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Assertion (A):</strong> The carbonate of lithium decomposes easily on heating to form lithium oxide and \(\mathrm{CO_2}\).</p>
<p><strong>Reason (R):</strong> Lithium being very small in size polarises large carbonate ion leading to the formation of more stable \(\mathrm{Li_2O}\) and \(\mathrm{CO_2}\).</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>अभिकथन (A):</strong> लीथियम का कार्बोनेट गर्म करने पर आसानी से विघटित होकर लीथियम ऑक्साइड तथा \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) बनाता है।</p>
<p><strong>कारण (R):</strong> लीथियम का आकार अत्यंत छोटा होने के कारण यह बड़े कार्बोनेट आयन का ध्रुवण करता है, जिससे अधिक स्थायी \(\mathrm{Li_2O}\) और \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) का निर्माण होता है।</p>]]></text></questiontext>

<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of the Statements</strong><br/>Unlike the carbonates of other alkali metals, lithium carbonate (\(\mathrm{Li_2CO_3}\)) is thermally unstable and decomposes readily upon heating to yield lithium oxide (\(\mathrm{Li_2O}\)) and carbon dioxide (\(\mathrm{CO_2}\)):<br/>\[\mathrm{Li_2CO_3} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{Li_2O} + \mathrm{CO_2}\]<br/>This occurs because the \(\mathrm{Li}^+\) ion is very small and possesses high polarising power.
