37. **At constant volume**

By first law of thermodynamics:

$$
\begin{array}{l} q = \Delta U + (- w) \\ (- w) = p \Delta V \\ \therefore \quad q = \Delta U + p \Delta V \\ \Delta V = 0, \text { since volume is constant.} \\ \therefore \quad q _ {V} = \Delta U + 0 \\ \Rightarrow q _ {V} = \Delta U = \text { change in internal energy} \\ \end{array}
$$

**At constant pressure**

$$
q _ {p} = \Delta U + p \Delta V
$$

But, $\Delta U + p\Delta V = \Delta H$

$$
\therefore \quad q _ {p} = \Delta H = \text { change in enthalpy.}
$$

So, at a constant volume and at constant pressure heat change is a state function because it is equal to change in internal energy and change in enthalpy respectively which are state functions.
