    <text><![CDATA[<p>The element which exists in liquid state for a wide range of temperature and can be used for measuring high temperature is</p>
<hr/>
<p>যে মৌলটি তাপমাত্রার একটি বিস্তৃত পরিসরে তরল অবস্থায় বিদ্যমান থাকে এবং উচ্চ তাপমাত্রা পরিমাপের জন্য ব্যবহৃত হতে পারে, তা হলো</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Physical Properties of Group 13 Elements</strong><br/>
Gallium (\(\mathrm{Ga}\)) has an unusually low melting point of \(303\text{ K}\) (\(30^\circ\text{C}\)) due to its crystal structure consisting of discrete \(\mathrm{Ga}_2\) molecules, while its boiling point is remarkably high at approximately \(2676\text{ K}\) (\(2403^\circ\text{C}\)).</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Technological Application</strong><br/>
Because gallium remains in the liquid state over a vast range of nearly \(2373\text{ K}\) and exhibits very low vapor pressure even at high temperatures, it is ideally suited for measuring high temperatures in quartz thermometers.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Answer</strong><br/>
The element is Gallium (\(\mathrm{Ga}\)).</p>
<hr/>

    <text><![CDATA[<p>Which of the following is a Lewis acid?</p>
<hr/>

  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle of Lewis Acids</strong><br/>
A Lewis acid is defined as a chemical species capable of accepting a pair of electrons due to an incomplete octet or an accessible empty orbital.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of the Given Chlorides</strong><br/>
In aluminium chloride (\(\mathrm{AlCl}_3\)), aluminium has only six electrons in its outermost valence shell, leaving an incomplete octet and a vacant \(3p\) orbital available to accept an electron pair. Conversely, magnesium chloride (\(\mathrm{MgCl}_2\)), calcium chloride (\(\mathrm{CaCl}_2\)), and barium chloride (\(\mathrm{BaCl}_2\)) are ionic chlorides of Group 2 metals possessing stable noble-gas electronic configurations.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Answer</strong><br/>
The compound that behaves as a Lewis acid is \(\mathrm{AlCl}_3\).</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>ধাপ ১: লুইস অ্যাসিডের ধারণাগত নীতি</strong><br/>

  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Electronic Configuration and Orbital Hybridisation</strong><br/>
Beryllium has an atomic number of \(4\) with electronic configuration \(1s^2\,2s^2\). In coordinating with four hydroxide ligands, the central beryllium atom provides one \(2s\) orbital and three \(2p\) orbitals, which mix together to produce four equivalent \(sp^3\) hybrid orbitals.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Coordination Geometry</strong><br/>
Four \(sp^3\) hybrid orbitals are directed toward the vertices of a regular tetrahedron with bond angles of approximately \(109.5^\circ\). Therefore, the geometry of the complex is tetrahedral.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Answer</strong><br/>
The hybridisation is \(sp^3\) and the geometry is tetrahedral.</p>

    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Periodic Variation of Acid-Base Character in Group 13</strong><br/>
Moving down Group 13, the non-metallic character decreases while the metallic character increases due to the decreasing ionization enthalpy and increasing atomic size. As a result, the oxides change progressively from acidic to amphoteric and then to basic.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Chemical Character of the Oxides</strong><br/>
Boron trioxide (\(\mathrm{B}_2\mathrm{O}_3\)) is non-metallic and acidic; it reacts with basic metal oxides to form borates and dissolves in water to form boric acid. Aluminium oxide (\(\mathrm{Al}_2\mathrm{O}_3\)) and gallium oxide (\(\mathrm{Ga}_2\mathrm{O}_3\)) are amphoteric, whereas indium oxide (\(\mathrm{In}_2\mathrm{O}_3\)) and thallium oxide are basic.</p>

Because the second shell has no \(d\)-orbitals, only four valence orbitals (one \(2s\) and three \(2p\)) are available. Consequently, boron cannot expand its octet beyond a covalency of \(4\), restricting its coordination number to at most \(4\) (such as in \(\mathrm{BF}_4^-\)).</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Availability of d-Orbitals in Heavier Group 13 Elements</strong><br/>
Aluminium (\(\mathrm{Al}\)), gallium (\(\mathrm{Ga}\)), and indium (\(\mathrm{In}\)) possess vacant \(d\)-orbitals in their valence shells, allowing them to expand their coordination number to \(6\) and form octahedral complexes of the formula \(\mathrm{MF}_6^{3-}\).</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Answer</strong><br/>
The element that is not likely to act as the central atom in \(\mathrm{MF}_6^{3-}\) is Boron (\(\mathrm{B}\)).</p>

Linear silicone polymers are prepared by the hydrolysis of dichlorosilanes, \(\mathrm{Me}_2\mathrm{SiCl}_2\), followed by condensation polymerization of the resulting dimethylsilanediol, \(\mathrm{Me}_2\mathrm{Si(OH)}_2\). Each silanediol monomer possesses two terminal \(\mathrm{-OH}\) groups, allowing continuous linear chain propagation at both ends.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Chain Termination Mechanism</strong><br/>
When chlorotrimethylsilane, \(\mathrm{Me}_3\mathrm{SiCl}\), is added, its hydrolysis gives trimethylsilanol, \(\mathrm{Me}_3\mathrm{SiOH}\). Because \(\mathrm{Me}_3\mathrm{SiOH}\) possesses only one reactive \(\mathrm{-OH}\) group, condensation of this unit at the active terminus of a growing silicone chain caps the end, preventing further growth. Thus, the chain length of the silicone polymer is effectively controlled.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Answer</strong><br/>
The chain length is controlled by adding \(\mathrm{Me}_3\mathrm{SiCl}\).</p>

  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Origin of the Anomaly in Atomic Radii</strong><br/>
Aluminium has an atomic radius of \(143\text{ pm}\), whereas gallium has an atomic radius of \(135\text{ pm}\). Across the transition series preceding gallium, ten electrons enter the inner \(3d\) subshell (\(3d^{10}\)).</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Poor Shielding and High Effective Nuclear Charge</strong><br/>
Because \(d\) orbitals are diffuse and spatially dispersed, they exert very poor screening on the outer valence electrons. Concurrently, the nuclear charge increases by \(10\) units (from atomic number \(21\) to \(30\)). The combined effect of poor screening of \(d\) orbitals and the marked increase in nuclear charge exerts a stronger electrostatic pull on the outer valence electrons, pulling them inward and making the radius of gallium smaller than that of aluminium.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Answer</strong><br/>
The reasons are poor screening effect of d and f orbitals and increase in nuclear charge.</p>

In a carbon dioxide (\(\mathrm{CO}_2\)) molecule, the central carbon atom forms two \(\sigma\)-bonds with two oxygen atoms using two equivalent \(sp\) hybrid orbitals. These two \(sp\) orbitals are oriented at an angle of \(180^\circ\) to minimize electrostatic repulsion, conferring a strictly linear molecular framework.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Pi-Bonding Mechanism</strong><br/>
The remaining two unhybridised \(2p\) orbitals of carbon overlap laterally with corresponding \(2p\) orbitals of the oxygen atoms to form two \(p\pi-p\pi\) multiple bonds. Thus, both \(sp\) hybridisation of carbon and \(p\pi-p\pi\) bonding between carbon and oxygen account for the stable linear structure of carbon dioxide.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Answer</strong><br/>
The linear shape is due to \(sp\) hybridisation of carbon and \(p\pi - p\pi\) bonding between carbon and oxygen.</p>

Standard thermodynamic measurements define the standard enthalpy of formation of graphite as zero (\(\Delta_{\mathrm{f}}H^\circ = 0\)), making graphite the thermodynamically most stable allotrope of carbon at ambient conditions. Additionally, graphite is soft and slippery because of weak van der Waals forces holding its adjacent graphene sheets together, allowing them to glide over one another; it is not hard (diamond is the hard allotrope of carbon).</p>

    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Electronic Configuration and Octet Rule for Carbon Dioxide</strong><br/>
Carbon dioxide (\(\mathrm{CO}_2\)) contains \(16\) valence electrons. In valid canonical contributing forms, all three atoms must satisfy the octet rule wherever possible, maintain the linear atomic arrangement, and exhibit appropriate formal charges.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Canonical Contributing Structures</strong><br/>
The primary non-polar canonical structure is the symmetrical double-bonded form \(\mathrm{O = C = O}\), where each atom has a formal charge of zero and a completed octet. Charge-separated canonical contributors involve shifting an electron pair to form a triple bond between carbon and one oxygen while a single bond exists with the other oxygen bearing a negative charge and the triple-bonded oxygen bearing a positive formal charge, giving \(^{-}\mathrm{O - C \equiv O}^{+}\) and \(^{+}\mathrm{O \equiv C - O}^{-}\). Structures lacking formal charges on single/triple bonded forms violate formal charge conservation and octet requirements.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Answer</strong><br/>
The correct canonical resonance structures are \(\mathrm{O = C = O}\) and \(^{-}\mathrm{O - C \equiv O}^{+}\).</p>
