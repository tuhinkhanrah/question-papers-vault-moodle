  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p>Which of the processes described below are irreversible?</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>A thermodynamic process is reversible only if it is carried out quasi-statically (infinitely slowly through a continuous succession of equilibrium states) and in the complete absence of dissipative effects such as friction, viscosity, inelastic deformation, or spontaneous finite temperature differences.<br/>Any process involving dissipation of mechanical work into internal thermal energy, sudden finite unrestrained expansion/compression, or heat conduction across a finite non-zero temperature difference is inherently irreversible according to the Second Law of Thermodynamics.</p><p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Mechanical Hammering</strong><br/>When an iron rod is hammered, mechanical work performed on the rod causes plastic (inelastic) deformation and internal dissipative friction, which increases the internal thermal energy and temperature of the rod. Heat generated cannot spontaneously convert back into mechanical energy to reverse the deformation and un-hammer the rod. Hence, this process is irreversible.</p><p><strong>Step 3: Evaluation of Heat Transfer Across a Finite Temperature Difference</strong><br/>When a gas at temperature \(T_1\) is placed in direct contact with a large reservoir at a higher temperature \(T_2\), heat flows spontaneously across a finite temperature difference \(\Delta T = T_2 - T_1 > 0\). Heat cannot spontaneously flow back from the colder gas to the hotter reservoir without external work being done by the surroundings. Hence, spontaneous heat transfer through a finite temperature difference is an irreversible process.</p><p><strong>Step 4: Evaluation of Quasi-Static Expansion with Frictionless Piston</strong><br/>A quasi-static expansion occurs infinitely slowly so that the system remains in thermodynamic equilibrium at every intermediate stage. Because the piston is frictionless, there are no dissipative frictional forces. Therefore, the system and surroundings can retrace their exact path in reverse without leaving any net change in the universe. Hence, this process is reversible.</p><p><strong>Step 5: Evaluation of Sudden Adiabatic Compression by a Finite Weight and Final Conclusion</strong><br/>When a finite weight \(W\) is placed on the piston, the external pressure abruptly exceeds the internal gas pressure by a finite amount. The piston moves with non-zero acceleration, producing pressure waves, turbulence, and non-equilibrium states within the gas. Dissipation occurs, and removing the weight does not spontaneously return both the gas and surroundings to their initial equilibrium states. Hence, this sudden compression process is irreversible.<br/>Therefore, the processes that are irreversible are: the increase in temperature of an iron rod by hammering it; a gas in a small container brought into contact with a reservoir at a higher temperature; and the sudden compression of gas caused by adding a weight to the piston.</p>]]></text>
