  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[
    <p><strong>Step 1: Given Data and Conditions</strong><br/>Two vectors are given in Cartesian component form as \(\mathbf{A} = \hat{\mathbf{i}} + \hat{\mathbf{j}}\) and \(\mathbf{B} = \hat{\mathbf{i}} - \hat{\mathbf{j}}\).</p>
    <p><strong>Step 2: Assumptions and Sign Conventions</strong><br/>The unit vectors \(\hat{\mathbf{i}}\) and \(\hat{\mathbf{j}}\) are mutually orthogonal unit vectors along the positive \(X\)-axis and \(Y\)-axis, respectively, so \(\hat{\mathbf{i}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{i}} = \hat{\mathbf{j}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{j}} = 1\) and \(\hat{\mathbf{i}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{j}} = 0\).     <p><strong>Step 3: Relevant Formulae and Principles</strong><br/>The dot product of two vectors \(\mathbf{A}\) and \(\mathbf{B}\) is given by \(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = |\mathbf{A}||\mathbf{B}|\cos\theta\), so \(\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B}}{|\mathbf{A}||\mathbf{B}|}\).</p>
    <p><strong>Step 4: Step-by-Step Working</strong><br/>Compute the dot product: \(\mathbf{A} \cdot \mathbf{B} = (1)(1) + (1)(-1) = 1 - 1 = 0\).<br/>Compute the magnitudes: \(|\mathbf{A}| = \sqrt{1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2}\) and \(|\mathbf{B}| = \sqrt{1^2 + (-1)^2} = \sqrt{2}\).<br/>Substitute into the cosine formula: \(\cos\theta = \frac{0}{\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2}} = 0\).<br/>For \(0^\circ \leq \theta \leq 180^\circ\), \(\cos\theta = 0\) gives \(\theta = 90^\circ\).</p>
    <p><strong>Step 5: Check and Final Answer</strong><br/>The vectors lie along \(45^\circ\) above and \(45^\circ\) below the positive \(X\)-axis, so the angle between them is indeed \(90^\circ\). The final answer is \(90^\circ\).</p>
