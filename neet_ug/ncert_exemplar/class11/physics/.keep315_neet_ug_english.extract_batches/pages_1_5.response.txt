  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p>A string of mass \( 2.5\text{ kg} \) is under a tension of \( 200\text{ N} \). The length of the stretched string is \( 20.0\text{ m} \). If the transverse jerk is struck at one end of the string, the disturbance will reach the other end in</p>]]></text>

  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Given Data and Conditions</strong><br/>Total mass of the stretched string: \( M = 2.5\text{ kg} \)<br/>Length of the string: \( L = 20.0\text{ m} \)<br/>Tension applied to the string: \( T = 200\text{ N} \)</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Physical Model</strong><br/>A transverse wave on a stretched string propagates under tension. The string is assumed uniform with constant linear mass density.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Relevant Formulae</strong><br/>1. Linear mass density (mass per unit length):<br/>\[ \mu = \frac{M}{L} \]<br/>2. Time taken by the disturbance to traverse length \( L \):<br/>\[ t = \frac{L}{v} = \frac{L}{\sqrt{\frac{T L}{M}}} = \sqrt{\frac{M L}{T}} \]</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Step-by-Step Working</strong><br/>Calculate the linear mass density:<br/>\[ \mu = \frac{2.5\text{ kg}}{20.0\text{ m}} = 0.125\text{ kg/m} \]<br/>Calculate the wave speed:<br/>\[ v = \sqrt{\frac{200\text{ N}}{0.125\text{ kg/m}}} = \sqrt{1600} = 40\text{ m/s} \]<br/>Calculate the transit time across the string:<br/>\[ t = \frac{L}{v} = \frac{20.0\text{ m}}{40\text{ m/s}} = 0.5\text{ s} \]<br/>which corresponds to half a second.</p>
<p><strong>Step 5: Check and Final Answer</strong><br/>Units are consistent: \( \text{m} / (\text{m/s}) = \text{s} \). The disturbance will reach the other end in 0.5 second.</p>]]></text>

  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Given Conditions and Physical System</strong><br/>A source of sound (train) emitting sound of original frequency \( n_0 \) travels along a straight track at a constant speed \( V \). A stationary observer is positioned on the platform. The train approaches the observer, passes the observer, and recedes away along the track.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Principles of Doppler Effect</strong><br/>Let \( v \) represent the speed of sound in still air.<br/>1. During approach: The source moves towards the stationary observer at constant speed \( V \). The apparent frequency \( n_{\text{app}} \) heard by the observer is:<br/>\[ n_{\text{app}} = n_0 \left( \frac{v}{v - V} \right) \]<br/>Because \( v \), \( V \), and \( n_0 \) are all constants, the apparent frequency is constant over time and is strictly greater than \( n_0 \).<br/>2. During recession: After passing the observer, the source moves away at constant speed \( V \). The apparent frequency \( n_{\text{rec}} \) heard by the observer is:<br/>\[ n_{\text{rec}} = n_0 \left( \frac{v}{v + V} \right) \]<br/>Because all terms are constant, the apparent frequency is constant over time and is strictly less than \( n_0 \).<br/>3. Transition at the observer: At the instant the train passes the observer, the observed frequency drops abruptly from the higher constant value \( n_{\text{app}} \) to the lower constant value \( n_{\text{rec}} \).</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Plot Evaluation and Final Conclusion</strong><br/>The plot of apparent frequency \( n' \) as a function of time \( t \) must display a horizontal straight line at a higher constant value during the approach, followed by an abrupt step drop at the instant the train passes the observer, and continuing as a horizontal straight line at a lower constant value during recession. The curve featuring a high constant value dropping discontinuously to a lower constant value accurately depicts this behavior.</p>]]></text>
