  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Given Data and Conditions</strong><br/>Diameter of the first pipe section, \(d_1 = 2.5\text{ cm}\).<br/>Diameter of the second pipe section, \(d_2 = 3.75\text{ cm}\).</p><p><strong>Step 2: Assumptions and Sign Conventions</strong><br/>The fluid is ideal (incompressible and non-viscous) and flows steadily through a circular pipe without any sources or sinks.</p><p><strong>Step 3: Relevant Formulae and Principles</strong><br/>By the equation of continuity for an incompressible fluid, the volume flow rate is constant:<br/>\[A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2\]<br/>where \(A = \frac{\pi d^2}{4}\) is the cross-sectional area of a circular pipe of diameter \(d\).</p><p><strong>Step 4: Step-by-Step Working</strong><br/>\[\frac{v_1}{v_2} = \frac{A_2}{A_1} = \frac{\pi d_2^2 / 4}{\pi d_1^2 / 4} = \left(\frac{d_2}{d_1}\right)^2\]<br/>Substituting the given diameters:<br/>\[\frac{v_1}{v_2} = \left(\frac{3.75\text{ cm}}{2.5\text{ cm}}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{9}{4}\]</p><p><strong>Step 5: Check and Final Answer</strong><br/>Since \(d_1 &lt; d_2\), we must have \(v_1 &gt; v_2\), which agrees with \(9:4\).
