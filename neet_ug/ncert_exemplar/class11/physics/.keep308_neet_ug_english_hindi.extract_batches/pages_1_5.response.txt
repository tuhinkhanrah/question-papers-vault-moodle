<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Uniformity and Direction</strong><br/>When the density distribution inside a sphere is non-uniform, the mass is not spherically symmetric. Consequently, the resultant gravitational pull does not necessarily point directly towards the geometric centre of the earth, nor does its magnitude remain identical at all points on the surface.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Evaluation of Zero-Gravity Possibility</strong><br/>For any point on the surface of a spherical body, all mass elements of the earth lie on one side of the tangent plane drawn at that surface point. Therefore, every mass element produces an inward component of gravitational force along the normal to the surface. Since all these inward components add up constructively in the same half-space, the net acceleration due to gravity can never cancel out to zero at any point on the surface.</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Thus, on the surface of a non-uniformly dense spherical earth, the acceleration due to gravity cannot be zero at any point.</p>

<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of the Sun-Earth and Mercury-Sun Systems</strong><br/>Because the earth revolves around the sun in an approximately circular orbit under the sun's gravitational pull, from the earth's reference frame the sun appears to trace an approximately circular path around the earth.   <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>For a spherically symmetric mass distribution (a uniform density sphere), the net gravitational force exerted by an external point mass or spherical body (such as the sun) acts along the line joining the centres of the two bodies.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Torque about the Centre of Mass</strong><br/>The centre of mass (c.m.) of a uniform density sphere coincides with its geometric centre. Since the line of action of the resultant gravitational force exerted by the sun passes directly through the c.m. of the earth, the perpendicular distance (lever arm) from the c.m. to the line of action of the force is zero. Alternatively, by symmetry about the line joining the centres of the sun and the earth, the torques due to pairs of symmetrically situated mass elements on opposite sides of this line cancel out completely.</p>

<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Satellites have a finite lifetime because viscous forces cause their speed (energy) and hence orbital height to gradually decrease.</p>

<p><strong>Step 4: Step-by-Step Working</strong><br/>The gravitational force on particle \(m\) at \(B\) due to mass \(2M\) at \(A\) (directed towards \(A\)) is:<br/>\(F_{BA} = \frac{G (2M) m}{r^2} = \frac{2GMm}{r^2}\)<br/>The gravitational force on particle \(m\) at \(B\) due to mass \(M\) at \(C\) (directed towards \(C\)) is:<br/>\(F_{BC} = \frac{G M m}{(2r)^2} = \frac{GMm}{4r^2}\)<br/>Comparing the two magnitudes:<br/>\(F_{BA} - F_{BC} = \frac{2GMm}{r^2} - \frac{GMm}{4r^2} = \frac{7GMm}{4r^2} > 0\)<br/>Since \(F_{BA} > F_{BC}\), the net force on \(m\) is directed towards \(A\) (mass \(2M\)). <p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Altitude, Depth, Latitude, and Mass Dependence</strong><br/>1. <p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Orbits, Projectile Motion, and Shell Theorem</strong><br/>1. Closed elliptical orbits occur only for inverse-square (\(1/r^2\)) or linear Hookean (\(r\)) central forces (Bertrand's theorem). Near the surface of the earth (height \(h \ll R\)), the acceleration due to gravity \(g' = \frac{GM'}{R^3}\) remains approximately constant in magnitude and downward in direction, so the trajectory of a stone thrown by hand will still be approximately parabolic.<br/>4. Newton's shell theorem (zero gravitational force inside a uniform spherical shell) holds strictly only for the inverse-square law because the surface area of a solid angle scales as \(r^2\), cancelling \(1/r^2\). For an inverse-cube law, the force inside a uniform spherical shell will not be zero.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>If gravitation followed an inverse-cube law, planets would not have elliptic orbits, and projectile motion of a stone near the earth's surface would still be approximately parabolic.</p>

<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of CM and CG Separation</strong><br/>1. If the body is taken deep inside the earth, the gravitational field changes, so the CG can shift slightly, but the CM is an intrinsic property of the body's mass distribution and does not change.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>The centre of mass and centre of gravity of an extended body on the surface of the earth are close to each other for objects of sizes less than \(100\text{ m}\).</p>
