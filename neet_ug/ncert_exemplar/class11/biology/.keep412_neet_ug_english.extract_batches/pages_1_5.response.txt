Actively mobilized elements are translocated from older, senescing leaves to younger growing regions, so their deficiency appears first in older leaves. Relatively immobile elements cannot be easily remobilized from older tissues, so their deficiency appears first in young leaves.</p><p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation</strong><br/>Nitrogen, potassium, and magnesium are actively mobilized elements in plants; hence, their deficiency symptoms appear first in older leaves. In contrast, elements such as sulphur and calcium form part of the structural components of cells and are relatively immobile.</p><p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Because sulphur is relatively immobile, its deficiency symptoms tend to appear first in young leaves.</p>]]></text></generalfeedback>
