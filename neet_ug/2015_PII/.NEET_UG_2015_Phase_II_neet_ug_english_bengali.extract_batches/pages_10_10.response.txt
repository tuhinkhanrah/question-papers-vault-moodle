  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>
Bacteria possess various cell surface appendages including flagella, pili, and fimbriae, each serving distinct biological functions.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Functional Identification</strong><br/>
Fimbriae are small, bristle-like protein fibres sprouting out of the bacterial cell surface. They are known to enable bacteria to adhere firmly to solid substrates like rocks in aquatic streams and to epithelial surfaces of host tissues. Mesosomes are internal plasma membrane infoldings involved in respiration and wall formation, while rhizoids and holdfasts are anchoring organs found in non-vascular plants and algae.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
The specialized structures enabling bacteria to attach to rocks and host tissues are fimbriae.</p>

<p><strong>Step 2: Ecological Phenomenon Identification</strong><br/>
Because these toxic chemicals are lipophilic and poorly degraded, their concentration in body tissues progressively multiplies as they move along successive trophic levels of a food chain. This phenomenon is defined as biomagnification (or biological magnification).</p>

    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Diagnostic Features of Animal Phyla</strong><br/>
Phylum Porifera (sponges) is distinguished by a primitive cellular grade of body organization where cells form a loose meshwork without true tissues or organs.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Aquiferous Canal System and Choanocytes</strong><br/>
Sponges possess a unique water canal system. Water enters through minute pores (ostia) into a central spongocoel or canal system lined with specialized flagellated collar cells called choanocytes that filter food particles. Sponges typically exhibit indirect development with a free-swimming larval stage (such as an amphiblastula or parenchymula) distinct from the adult.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
These diagnostic traits uniquely define members of phylum Porifera.</p>

  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Photolysis of Water in Photosynthesis</strong><br/>
During the light reactions of photosynthesis, the oxygen-evolving complex (OEC) associated with Photosystem II on the inner luminal side of the thylakoid membrane catalyzes the splitting of water:
\[2\mathrm{H_2O} \longrightarrow 4\mathrm{H^+} + 4\mathrm{e^-} + \mathrm{O_2}\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Essential Catalytic Cofactors</strong><br/>
The catalytic metal cluster of the oxygen-evolving complex requires manganese (\(\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}\)) and chloride (\(\mathrm{Cl^-}\)) ions (along with calcium) as essential inorganic cofactors to facilitate electron abstraction and oxygen evolution.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
The elements directly involved in the photolysis of water are manganese and chlorine.</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>ধাপ ১: সালোকসংশ্লেষে জলের আলোক-বিশ্লেষণ</strong><br/>
সালোকসংশ্লেষের আলোক দশায় থাইলাকয়েড পর্দার ভেতরের দিকে অবস্থিত সালোকতন্ত্র II (PS II)-এর সাথে যুক্ত অক্সিজেন নির্গমনকারী কমপ্লেক্স (OEC) জলের আলোক-বিশ্লেষণ বা ফটোলাইসিস সম্পন্ন করে:
\[2\mathrm{H_2O} \longrightarrow 4\mathrm{H^+} + 4\mathrm{e^-} + \mathrm{O_2}\]</p>
<p><strong>ধাপ ২: প্রয়োজনীয় মৌল উপাদান</strong><br/>
এই অক্সিজেন নির্গমনকারী উৎসেচক কমপ্লেক্সের সক্রিয়তার জন্য ম্যাঙ্গানিজ (\(\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}\)) এবং ক্লোরিন (\(\mathrm{Cl^-}\)) আয়ন প্রধান অজৈব কোফ্যাক্টর হিসেবে অপরিহার্য।</p>

<p><strong>Step 2: Cellular vs Acellular Layers</strong><br/>

  <li><strong>Cohesion:</strong> Strong mutual hydrogen bonding attraction between adjacent water molecules.</li>
  <li><strong>Adhesion:</strong> Attraction between water molecules and hydrophilic lignified xylem walls.</li>
  <li><strong>Tensile Strength:</strong> The intrinsic high tensile strength of water conferring tremendous resistance to a pulling force and preventing cavitation.</li>

<ul>

  <li>Predation: (+ / -) One species benefits, the other is killed.</li>

  <li>Competition: (- / -) Both competing species are adversely affected.</li>
</ul></p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Dynamics of Competition</strong><br/>
In competition, co-occurring organisms vie for the same limited ecological resources (such as nutrients, space, light, or water). Even the superior competitor expends vital metabolic energy and experiences reduced reproductive fitness compared to life in the absence of the competitor.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
Both interacting species are adversely affected in competition.</p>

  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p>A pleiotropic gene</p>
<hr/>
<p>একটি প্লিওট্রপিক জিন (pleiotropic gene)</p>]]></text>

  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Classical Genetics vs Pleiotropy</strong><br/>
Under simple Mendelian inheritance, one gene influences a single phenotypic trait. However, pleiotropy represents a phenomenon where a single gene product affects multiple metabolic pathways.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Phenotypic Manifestation</strong><br/>
Consequently, a pleiotropic gene simultaneously regulates or influences multiple distinct and seemingly unrelated phenotypic characters within an organism (e.g., the gene responsible for phenylketonuria in humans influences mental ability, skin pigmentation, and hair color).</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>
A pleiotropic gene controls multiple traits in an individual.</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>ধাপ ১: বংশগতিবিদ্যা ও প্লিওট্রপির সংজ্ঞা</strong><br/>
