      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Functional Roles of lac Operon Genes</strong><br/>The lactose operon consists of a regulatory gene and three structural genes whose products function cooperatively in lactose metabolism:<br/>
- The \(i\) gene (inhibitor/regulator gene) constitutively transcribes and translates the *lac* repressor protein (iv).<br/>
- The \(z\) gene encodes the enzyme \(\beta\)-galactosidase, which cleaves lactose into glucose and galactose (iii).<br/>
- The \(y\) gene encodes permease, an integral membrane protein that facilitates the entry of lactose into the cell (ii).<br/>
- The \(a\) gene encodes \(\beta\)-galactoside transacetylase, an enzyme that transfers an acetyl group from acetyl-CoA to \(\beta\)-galactosides (i).</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>The correct match is: (a) corresponds to (iv), (b) corresponds to (iii), (c) corresponds to (ii), and (d) corresponds to (i), giving (a) - (iv), (b) - (iii), (c) - (ii), (d) - (i).</p>

      <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Evaluation of Statement I</strong><br/>- Sickle cell anaemia is an autosomal recessive genetic disorder caused by a point mutation in the \(\beta\)-globin gene on chromosome 11.<br/>
- Haemophilia is a sex-linked (X-linked) recessive genetic disorder caused by a deficiency of clotting factors (Factor VIII in haemophilia A or Factor IX in haemophilia B).<br/>
Neither disorder is inherited as an autosomal dominant trait. Therefore, Statement I is incorrect.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Statement II</strong><br/>- Sickle cell anaemia impairs erythrocytes (red blood cells), where mutant hemoglobin polymerizes under low oxygen tension, causing RBCs to become sickle-shaped and leading to severe hemolytic anaemia and vaso-occlusion.<br/>
- Haemophilia affects the blood coagulation cascade, impairing the normal blood clotting process and leading to prolonged bleeding from wounds.<br/>
Both conditions are classical clinical disorders of the blood. Therefore, Statement II is correct.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Statement I is incorrect but Statement II is correct.</p>
