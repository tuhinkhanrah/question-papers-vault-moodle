- <strong>Synthesis gas</strong> is a mixture of \(\mathrm{CO}\) and \(\mathrm{H}_2\) and is used in the synthesis of methanol.<br/>
- <strong>Dihydrogen</strong> acts as a reducing agent, is prepared in the laboratory by reacting \(\mathrm{Zn} + \mathrm{NaOH}\) or \(\mathrm{Zn} + \text{dil. }\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{SO}_4\), and is used in the synthesis of methanol.<br/>
- <strong>Heavy water</strong> (\(\mathrm{D}_2\mathrm{O}\)) is prepared by the prolonged electrolysis of water.<br/>
- <strong>Calgon</strong> is sodium hexametaphosphate, \(\mathrm{Na}_2[\mathrm{Na}_4(\mathrm{PO}_3)_6]\), which is used for the softening of hard water.<br/>
- <strong>Hydrogen peroxide</strong> (\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\)) acts as both an oxidising agent and a reducing agent.<br/>
- <strong>Salt like hydrides</strong> (ionic/saline hydrides) are stoichiometric compounds formed by s-block elements with dihydrogen.</p>

  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[
<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>Each hydrogen-containing species has distinct physical and chemical applications based on its reactivity, bond energy, and redox properties.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Matches</strong><br/>
- <strong>Atomic hydrogen (\(\mathrm{H}\))</strong>: Produced by passing dihydrogen through an electric arc; it recombines on a metal surface to release a large amount of heat, so it can be used in cutting and welding.<br/>
- <strong>Dihydrogen (\(\mathrm{H}_2\))</strong>: Reacts with olefins and carbon monoxide in the hydroformylation of olefins to yield aldehydes, which are further reduced to alcohols.<br/>
- <strong>Water (\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\))</strong>: Can be reduced to dihydrogen by reaction with sodium hydride (\(\mathrm{NaH} + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} \rightarrow \mathrm{NaOH} + \mathrm{H}_2\)).<br/>
- <strong>Hydrogen peroxide (\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\))</strong>: Its \(30\%\) aqueous solution is marketed as an antiseptic under the name of perhydrol.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>The correct pairs are: \(\mathrm{H}\) – Can be used in cutting and welding; \(\mathrm{H}_2\) – Can be used in hydroformylation of olefin; \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\) – Can be reduced to dihydrogen by \(\mathrm{NaH}\); \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\) – Used in the name of perhydrol.</p>

- <strong>Atomic hydrogen</strong>: Highly reactive species that recombines on a metal surface to generate very high temperatures (around \(4000\text{ K}\)) used in welding.</p>

<p><strong>67.</strong> <strong>अभिकथन (A) :</strong> धावन सोडा के उपचार द्वारा जल की स्थायी कठोरता दूर की जाती है।</p>

]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[
<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle</strong><br/>Permanent hardness of water is caused by the presence of dissolved chlorides and sulphates of calcium and magnesium (\(\mathrm{CaCl}_2, \mathrm{MgCl}_2, \mathrm{CaSO}_4, \mathrm{MgSO}_4\)), which cannot be removed by simple boiling.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of the Assertion and Reason</strong><br/>
- Treating hard water with washing soda (\(\mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{CO}_3\)) removes permanent hardness because carbonate ions react with soluble \(\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}\) and \(\mathrm{Mg}^{2+}\) salts (such as sulphates and chlorides) to precipitate insoluble calcium carbonate (\(\mathrm{CaCO}_3\)) and magnesium carbonate (\(\mathrm{MgCO}_3\)):<br/>
\(\mathrm{MCl}_2 + \mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{CO}_3 \rightarrow \mathrm{MCO}_3 \downarrow + 2\mathrm{NaCl}\)<br/>
\(\mathrm{MSO}_4 + \mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{CO}_3 \rightarrow \mathrm{MCO}_3 \downarrow + \mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{SO}_4\) (where \(\mathrm{M} = \mathrm{Mg}, \mathrm{Ca}\)).<br/>
- Therefore, both the assertion and the reason are scientifically true, and the precipitation of insoluble carbonates directly explains why washing soda removes permanent hardness.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Final Conclusion</strong><br/>Both statements A and R are correct, and R is the correct explanation of A.</p>

\(\mathrm{MCl}_2 + \mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{CO}_3 \rightarrow \mathrm{MCO}_3 \downarrow + 2\mathrm{NaCl}\)<br/>
\(\mathrm{MSO}_4 + \mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{CO}_3 \rightarrow \mathrm{MCO}_3 \downarrow + \mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{SO}_4\) (जहाँ \(\mathrm{M} = \mathrm{Mg}, \mathrm{Ca}\))।<br/>
