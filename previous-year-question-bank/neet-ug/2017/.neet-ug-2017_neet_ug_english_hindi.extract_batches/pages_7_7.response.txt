    <text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_2017_MCQ_Q75 - Inert pair effect in group 14 elements</text>
  </name>
  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p>It is because of inability of \(ns^2\) electrons of the valence shell to participate in bonding that</p>
<hr/>
<p>संयोजी कोश के \(ns^2\) इलेक्ट्रॉनों के आबंधन में भाग लेने की असमर्थता के कारण ही :</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle of the Inert Pair Effect</strong><br/>
In heavier p-block elements (such as lead, \(\mathrm{Pb}\)), the valence \(s\)-electrons are poorly shielded from the nucleus by the intervening diffused \(d\)- and \(f\)-orbitals. As a consequence, the effective nuclear charge felt by the \(ns^2\) electron pair increases dramatically, making these electrons tightly held and reluctant to participate in chemical bonding. This phenomenon is known as the inert pair effect.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Relative Stability of Oxidation States in Group 14</strong><br/>
Group 14 elements exhibit \(+4\) and \(+2\) oxidation states.
- Down the group, the stability of the lower oxidation state (\(+2\)) increases, while that of the higher oxidation state (\(+4\)) decreases:
  \[\text{Stability of } +2: \mathrm{Ge^{2+} < Sn^{2+} < Pb^{2+}}\]
  \[\text{Stability of } +4: \mathrm{Ge^{4+} > Sn^{4+} > Pb^{4+}}\]
- For tin (\(\mathrm{Sn}\)), the \(+4\) oxidation state is more stable than \(+2\). Therefore, \(\mathrm{Sn^{2+}}\) readily loses two electrons to form \(\mathrm{Sn^{4+}}\), behaving as a strong reducing agent:
  \[\mathrm{Sn^{2+} \longrightarrow Sn^{4+} + 2e^- \quad (\text{reducing agent})}\]
- For lead (\(\mathrm{Pb}\)), due to the strong inert pair effect, the \(+2\) state is far more stable than the \(+4\) state. Consequently, \(\mathrm{Pb^{4+}}\) easily gains two electrons to reduce to the stable \(\mathrm{Pb^{2+}}\), behaving as a powerful oxidizing agent:
  \[\mathrm{Pb^{4+} + 2e^- \longrightarrow Pb^{2+} \quad (\text{oxidizing agent})}\]</p>
<p><strong>Final Conceptual Conclusion:</strong><br/>
Due to the inert pair effect, \(\mathrm{Sn^{2+}}\) acts as a reducing agent while \(\mathrm{Pb^{4+}}\) acts as an oxidizing agent.</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>चरण 1: अक्रिय युग्म प्रभाव का सिद्धांत</strong><br/>
भारी p-ब्लॉक तत्वों (जैसे लेड, \(\mathrm{Pb}\)) में आंतरिक \(d\)- और \(f\)-कक्षकों के दुर्बल परिरक्षण प्रभाव के कारण बाह्यतम कोश के \(ns^2\) इलेक्ट्रॉन नाभिक से अत्यधिक दृढ़ता से बंधे रहते हैं और रासायनिक आबंधन में भाग लेने के लिए उपलब्ध नहीं होते। इसे अक्रिय युग्म प्रभाव कहते हैं।</p>
<p><strong>चरण 2: वर्ग 14 में ऑक्सीकरण अवस्थाओं का स्थायित्व</strong><br/>
वर्ग में नीचे जाने पर निम्नतर (\(+2\)) ऑक्सीकरण अवस्था का स्थायित्व बढ़ता है तथा उच्चतर (\(+4\)) ऑक्सीकरण अवस्था का स्थायित्व घटता है:
- टिन (\(\mathrm{Sn}\)) के लिए \(+4\) अवस्था \(+2\) अवस्था की तुलना में अधिक स्थायी होती है। अतः \(\mathrm{Sn^{2+}}\) आसानी से \(\mathrm{Sn^{4+}}\) में ऑक्सीकृत होकर अपचायक की भांति व्यवहार करता है।
- लेड (\(\mathrm{Pb}\)) के लिए अक्रिय युग्म प्रभाव के कारण \(+2\) अवस्था अत्यधिक स्थायी होती है। अतः \(\mathrm{Pb^{4+}}\) आसानी से दो इलेक्ट्रॉन ग्रहण करके \(\mathrm{Pb^{2+}}\) में बदल जाता है और एक प्रबल ऑक्सीकारक का कार्य करता है।</p>
<p><strong>अंतिम संकल्पनात्मक निष्कर्ष:</strong><br/>
\(\mathrm{Sn^{2+}}\) अपचायक है जबकि \(\mathrm{Pb^{4+}}\) ऑक्सीकारक है।</p>]]></text>

<hr/>
<p>एक दी गई अभिक्रिया के लिए \(\Delta H = 35.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}\) तथा \(\Delta S = 83.6\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}\) है। अभिक्रिया किस ताप पर स्वतःप्रवर्तित होगी : [मान लीजिए कि \(\Delta H\) तथा \(\Delta S\) ताप के साथ परिवर्तित नहीं होते हैं]</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Given Thermodynamic Data</strong><br/>
- Enthalpy change: \(\Delta H = 35.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} = 35500\text{ J mol}^{-1}\)<br/>
- Entropy change: \(\Delta S = 83.6\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}\)</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Spontaneity Criterion (Gibbs-Helmholtz Equation)</strong><br/>
A reaction is spontaneous when the change in Gibbs free energy is negative:
\[\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S < 0\]
Rearranging for temperature \(T\):
\[T\Delta S > \Delta H \implies T > \frac{\Delta H}{\Delta S}\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Step-by-Step Calculation</strong><br/>
Substitute the numerical values:
\[T > \frac{35500\text{ J mol}^{-1}}{83.6\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}}\]
\[T > 424.64\text{ K} \approx 425\text{ K}\]</p>
<p><strong>Final Conceptual Conclusion:</strong><br/>
The reaction is spontaneous at temperatures \(T > 425\text{ K}\).</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>चरण 1: दिए गए मान</strong><br/>
- एन्थैल्पी परिवर्तन: \(\Delta H = 35.5\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} = 35500\text{ J mol}^{-1}\)<br/>
- एन्ट्रॉपी परिवर्तन: \(\Delta S = 83.6\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}\)</p>
<p><strong>चरण 2: स्वतःप्रवर्तिता की कसौटी</strong><br/>

\[\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S < 0\]
\[T > \frac{\Delta H}{\Delta S}\]</p>
<p><strong>चरण 3: ताप की गणना</strong><br/>

\[T > \frac{35500}{83.6} \approx 424.64\text{ K} \approx 425\text{ K}\]</p>
<p><strong>अंतिम संकल्पनात्मक निष्कर्ष:</strong><br/>

    <text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_2017_MCQ_Q77 - Dependence of molal depression constant on solution concentration</text>
  </name>
  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p>If molality of the dilute solution is doubled, the value of molal depression constant (\(K_f\)) will be</p>
<hr/>
<p>यदि तनु विलयन की मोललता दुगुनी कर दी जाए, तो मोलल अवनमन स्थिरांक (\(K_f\)) का मान होगा :</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Definition and Governing Principle of Molal Depression Constant</strong><br/>
The depression in freezing point of a solution is given by:
\[\Delta T_f = K_f \cdot m\]
where \(m\) is the molality of the solution and \(K_f\) is the molal depression constant (cryoscopic constant).</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Physical Dependence of \(K_f\)</strong><br/>
Thermodynamically, \(K_f\) depends exclusively on the properties of the solvent:
\[K_f = \frac{R \cdot M_1 \cdot T_f^2}{1000 \cdot \Delta_{\text{fus}}H}\]
where \(M_1\) is the molar mass of the solvent, \(T_f\) is the freezing point of the pure solvent, and \(\Delta_{\text{fus}}H\) is the enthalpy of fusion of the solvent.
Since \(K_f\) is a characteristic property of the solvent alone, changing the concentration (molality) of the solute doubles \(\Delta T_f\), but leaves the value of \(K_f\) completely unchanged.</p>
<p><strong>Final Conceptual Conclusion:</strong><br/>
The value of the molal depression constant (\(K_f\)) remains unchanged.</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>चरण 1: मोलल अवनमन स्थिरांक की परिभाषा</strong><br/>
हिमांक में अवनमन का सूत्र है:
\[\Delta T_f = K_f \cdot m\]
जहाँ \(m\) मोललता है तथा \(K_f\) मोलल अवनमन स्थिरांक है।</p>
<p><strong>चरण 2: \(K_f\) की विलायक पर निर्भरता</strong><br/>
मोलल अवनमन स्थिरांक \(K_f\) केवल विलायक की प्रकृति पर निर्भर करता है:
\[K_f = \frac{R \cdot M_1 \cdot T_f^2}{1000 \cdot \Delta_{\text{fus}}H}\]
यह विलेय की सांद्रता या मोललता पर निर्भर नहीं करता। विलयन की मोललता दोगुनी करने पर हिमांक में अवनमन (\(\Delta T_f\)) तो दोगुना हो जाता है, परन्तु \(K_f\) का मान पूर्णतः अपरिवर्तित रहता है।</p>
<p><strong>अंतिम संकल्पनात्मक निष्कर्ष:</strong><br/>
मोलल अवनमन स्थिरांक (\(K_f\)) का मान अपरिवर्तित रहेगा।</p>]]></text>

<question type="multichoice">
  <name>
    <text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_2017_MCQ_Q78 - Temperature dependent concentration unit identification</text>
  </name>
  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p>Which of the following is dependent on temperature ?</p>
<hr/>
<p>निम्नलिखित में से कौन ताप पर निर्भर करता है?</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Conceptual Principle of Concentration Units</strong><br/>
Concentration expressions that include a volume term are temperature-dependent because liquid volume expands or contracts with temperature variations. Expressions based strictly on mass (weight) are independent of temperature because mass remains invariant with temperature.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Evaluation of Options</strong><br/>
- Molarity is defined as the number of moles of solute dissolved per litre of solution:
  \[\text{Molarity} = \frac{\text{Moles of solute}}{\text{Volume of solution in litres}}\]
  Since volume changes with temperature, molarity is directly dependent on temperature.
- Molality is defined per mass of solvent (moles of solute per kg of solvent) and is temperature-independent.
- Weight percentage is a mass-to-mass ratio and is temperature-independent.
- Mole fraction is a ratio of moles (derived from mass) and is temperature-independent.</p>
<p><strong>Final Conceptual Conclusion:</strong><br/>
Molarity is dependent on temperature.</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>चरण 1: सांद्रता इकाइयों का ताप पर निर्भरता का सिद्धांत</strong><br/>
वे सांद्रता पद जिनमें विलयन का आयतन शामिल होता है, ताप पर निर्भर करते हैं क्योंकि ताप में परिवर्तन के साथ आयतन परिवर्तित होता है। द्रव्यमान पर आधारित सांद्रता पद ताप से अप्रभावित रहते हैं।</p>

- Frenkel defect (dislocation defect) is shown by ionic substances in which there is a very large difference in size between the cation and the anion, allowing the smaller cation to displace into an interstitial site (e.g., \(\mathrm{ZnS}\), \(\mathrm{AgCl}\), \(\mathrm{AgBr}\), \(\mathrm{AgI}\)).

Therefore, stating that Frenkel defect is favoured in compounds where cation and anion sizes are almost equal is incorrect.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Verification of Other Statements</strong><br/>
- In Schottky defect, missing ions leave vacancies in the crystal, causing an overall decrease in crystal density.
- Non-stoichiometric \(\mathrm{FeO_{0.98}}\) has a metal deficiency defect due to missing \(\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}\) ions being compensated by \(\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}\) ions.
- \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) solid has no free electrons and acts as an insulator, silicon has a narrow band gap and is a semiconductor, silver has metallic bonding and is a conductor, and quartz generates electricity under mechanical stress, exhibiting piezoelectricity.</p>

The statement claiming that Frenkel defect is favoured in ionic compounds in which sizes of cation and anions are almost equal is incorrect.</p>

Finely powdered silver ore or native silver is leached with a dilute solution of sodium cyanide (\(\mathrm{NaCN}\)) in the presence of air/oxygen to form the soluble dicyanoargentate(I) coordination complex:
\[\mathrm{4\,Ag + 8\,CN^- + 2\,H_2O + O_2 \longrightarrow 4\,[Ag(CN)_2]^- + 4\,OH^-}\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Recovery of Silver by Metal Displacement</strong><br/>
Silver is recovered from the solution by a hydrometallurgical single displacement (cementation) reaction using metallic zinc scrap. Because zinc is more electropositive than silver (\(E^\circ_{\mathrm{Zn^{2+}/Zn}} = -0.76\text{ V}\) vs \(E^\circ_{\mathrm{Ag^+/Ag}} = +0.80\text{ V}\)), zinc acts as a reducing agent, displacing silver from the complex:
\[\mathrm{2\,[Ag(CN)_2]^- + Zn \longrightarrow [Zn(CN)_4]^{2-} + 2\,Ag \downarrow}\]</p>
<p><strong>Final Conceptual Conclusion:</strong><br/>
Silver is recovered by displacement with zinc (\(\mathrm{Zn}\)).</p>

Organometallic compounds are classified according to the nature of the metal-carbon bond into:

2. \(\pi\)-bonded organometallics: The metal coordinates with the delocalized \(\pi\)-electrons of an unsaturated ligand such as cyclopentadienyl rings or alkenes (e.g., ferrocene \(\mathrm{Fe(\eta^5-C_5H_5)_2}\), cobaltocene \(\mathrm{Co(\eta^5-C_5H_5)_2}\), ruthenocene \(\mathrm{Ru(\eta^5-C_5H_5)_2}\), Zeise's salt).</p>

    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Energy Levels in Actinoids vs Lanthanoids</strong><br/>
In lanthanoids, the energy difference between the \(4f\) and \(5d\) subshells is relatively large, so only electrons from \(6s\) and occasionally \(5d\) participate in chemical bonding, limiting their oxidation states primarily to \(+3\) (with occasional \(+2\) and \(+4\)).</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Analysis of Actinoids Electronic Structure</strong><br/>
In actinoids, the energy difference between the \(5f\), \(6d\), and \(7s\) subshells is very small. Because the \(5f\), \(6d\), and \(7s\) levels have comparable energies, electrons from all three subshells can easily be promoted and participate in chemical bond formation. As a consequence, actinoids exhibit a much wider variety of oxidation states (ranging from \(+3\) up to \(+7\), as seen in neptunium and plutonium).</p>
<p><strong>Final Conceptual Conclusion:</strong><br/>
The greater range of oxidation states in actinoids is due to the comparable energies of the \(5f\), \(6d\), and \(7s\) energy levels.</p>

  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p>The element \(Z = 114\) has been discovered recently. It will belong to which of the following family group and electronic configuration ?</p>
<hr/>
<p>तत्व \(Z = 114\) की हाल ही में खोज हुई है। यह किस परिवार/वर्ग तथा इलेक्ट्रॉनिक विन्यास से संबंधित होगा?</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Aufbau Principle and Noble Gas Core</strong><br/>
The noble gas preceding atomic number 114 is radon (\(\mathrm{Rn}\)), with atomic number \(Z = 86\). The remaining number of electrons to be placed is:
\[114 - 86 = 28\text{ electrons}\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Filling Order of Orbitals in Period 7</strong><br/>
After radon (completing period 6), the order of filling for period 7 orbitals is:
1. \(7s\) subshell (holds 2 electrons) \(\rightarrow 86 + 2 = 88\)
2. \(5f\) subshell (holds 14 electrons) \(\rightarrow 88 + 14 = 102\)
3. \(6d\) subshell (holds 10 electrons) \(\rightarrow 102 + 10 = 112\)
4. \(7p\) subshell (receives the remaining 2 electrons) \(\rightarrow 112 + 2 = 114\)
Thus, the electronic configuration is:
\[[\mathrm{Rn}]\, 5f^{14} 6d^{10} 7s^2 7p^2\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Determining the Group/Family</strong><br/>
An element with a valence shell configuration of \(ns^2 np^2\) possesses 4 valence electrons.
\[\text{Group Number} = 10 + \text{valence electrons} = 10 + 4 = 14\]
Group 14 is the Carbon family (C, Si, Ge, Sn, Pb, and Flerovium, Fl).</p>
<p><strong>Final Conceptual Conclusion:</strong><br/>
Element \(Z = 114\) belongs to the Carbon family with the electronic configuration \([\mathrm{Rn}]\, 5f^{14} 6d^{10} 7s^2 7p^2\).</p>

<question type="multichoice">
  <name>
    <text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_2017_MCQ_Q88 - Time calculation for concentration decay in first order reaction</text>
  </name>
  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p>A first order reaction has a specific reaction rate of \(10^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}\). How much time will it take for 20 g of the reactant to reduce to 5 g ?</p>
<hr/>
<p>एक प्रथम कोटि की अभिक्रिया का विशिष्ट अभिक्रिया वेग \(10^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}\) है। अभिकारक के \(20\text{ g}\) को घटकर \(5\text{ g}\) होने में कितना समय लगेगा?</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Given Data</strong><br/>
- Specific reaction rate (rate constant): \(k = 10^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}\)<br/>
- Initial mass of reactant: \([R]_0 = 20\text{ g}\)<br/>
- Final mass of reactant: \([R]_t = 5\text{ g}\)</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Integrated Rate Equation for First Order Reactions</strong><br/>
The integrated rate law is:
\[t = \frac{2.303}{k} \log_{10}\left(\frac{[R]_0}{[R]_t}\right)\]
Alternatively, observe that the reactant reduces from \(20\text{ g}\) to \(10\text{ g}\) (1 half-life), and from \(10\text{ g}\) to \(5\text{ g}\) (a second half-life). Hence, exactly two half-lives (\(n = 2\)) are required:
\[t = 2 \times t_{1/2}\]</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Calculation of Half-Life and Total Time</strong><br/>
The half-life of a first-order process is:
\[t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{k} = \frac{0.693}{10^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}} = 69.3\text{ s}\]
Multiplying by two half-lives gives:
\[t = 2 \times 69.3\text{ s} = 138.6\text{ s}\]</p>
<p><strong>Final Conceptual Conclusion:</strong><br/>
The time required for the reactant to reduce from 20 g to 5 g is 138.6 seconds.</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>चरण 1: दिए गए मान</strong><br/>
- वेग स्थिरांक: \(k = 10^{-2}\text{ s}^{-1}\)<br/>
- प्रारंभिक मात्रा: \([R]_0 = 20\text{ g}\)<br/>
- अंतिम मात्रा: \([R]_t = 5\text{ g}\)</p>
<p><strong>चरण 2: अर्ध-आयु विधि</strong><br/>
अभिकारक \(20\text{ g}\) से \(10\text{ g}\) (प्रथम अर्ध-आयु) तथा \(10\text{ g}\) से \(5\text{ g}\) (द्वितीय अर्ध-आयु) में परिवर्तित होता है। अतः कुल 2 अर्ध-आयु काल आवश्यक हैं:
\[t = 2 \times t_{1/2}\]</p>
<p><strong>चरण 3: समय की गणना</strong><br/>
प्रथम कोटि के लिए अर्ध-आयु:
\[t_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{k} = \frac{0.693}{10^{-2}} = 69.3\text{ सेकंड}\]
कुल समय:
\[t = 2 \times 69.3 = 138.6\text{ सेकंड}\]</p>
<p><strong>अंतिम संकल्पनात्मक निष्कर्ष:</strong><br/>
अभिकारक को घटकर \(5\text{ g}\) होने में \(138.6\text{ सेकंड}\) का समय लगेगा।</p>]]></text>

<question type="multichoice">
  <name>
    <text>NEET_UG_CHEMISTRY_2017_MCQ_Q89 - Hydration and ionic mobility of alkali metal ions</text>
  </name>
  <questiontext format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p>Ionic mobility of which of the following alkali metal ions is lowest when aqueous solution of their salts are put under an electric field ?</p>
<hr/>
<p>जब इनके लवणों के जलीय विलयन को विद्युत क्षेत्र में रखा जाता है, तो निम्नलिखित क्षार धातु आयनों में से किसकी आयनिक गतिशीलता सबसे कम होती है?</p>]]></text>
  </questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html">
    <text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Charge Density and Degree of Hydration</strong><br/>
Among the alkali metal cations (\(\mathrm{Li^+}\), \(\mathrm{Na^+}\), \(\mathrm{K^+}\), \(\mathrm{Rb^+}\)), all carry a single positive charge (\(+1\)). Down the group, the gaseous ionic radius increases:
\[r_{\text{ionic}}: \mathrm{Li^+ < Na^+ < K^+ < Rb^+}\]
Lithium (\(\mathrm{Li^+}\)) has the smallest ionic size and therefore the highest charge density (\(\frac{\text{charge}}{\text{radius}}\)). Due to this concentrated positive charge, \(\mathrm{Li^+}\) exerts the strongest electrostatic attraction on polar water molecules, leading to the greatest extent of hydration.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Effective Hydrated Radius and Ionic Mobility</strong><br/>
Because of its extensive hydration shell, the hydrated radius of \(\mathrm{Li^+}\) is the largest among all alkali metal cations:
\[r_{\text{hydrated}}: \mathrm{Li^+(aq) > Na^+(aq) > K^+(aq) > Rb^+(aq)}\]
In an aqueous solution under an electric field, the ionic mobility (\(\mu\)) is inversely proportional to the effective hydrated radius according to Stokes' law:
\[\mu \propto \frac{1}{r_{\text{hydrated}}}\]
Because \(\mathrm{Li^+(aq)}\) has the largest hydrated radius and experiences the greatest frictional drag, it exhibits the lowest ionic mobility.</p>
<p><strong>Final Conceptual Conclusion:</strong><br/>
The lithium ion (\(\mathrm{Li}\)) has the lowest ionic mobility in aqueous solution.</p>
<hr/>
<p><strong>चरण 1: आवेश घनत्व तथा जलयोजन की मात्रा</strong><br/>
क्षार धातु धनायनों (\(\mathrm{Li^+}\), \(\mathrm{Na^+}\), \(\mathrm{K^+}\), \(\mathrm{Rb^+}\)) में लिथियम आयन (\(\mathrm{Li^+}\)) का आकार सबसे छोटा होता है, जिससे इसका आवेश घनत्व सर्वाधिक होता है। उच्च आवेश घनत्व के कारण यह जल के अणुओं को अत्यधिक आकर्षित करता है और इसका जलयोजन सबसे अधिक होता है।</p>
<p><strong>चरण 2: जलयोजित त्रिज्या एवं आयनिक गतिशीलता</strong><br/>
अत्यधिक जलयोजन के कारण जलयोजित \(\mathrm{Li^+}\) आयन की प्रभावी त्रिज्या सबसे बड़ी हो जाती है:
\[\text{जलयोजित त्रिज्या}: \mathrm{Li^+(aq) > Na^+(aq) > K^+(aq) > Rb^+(aq)}\]
जलीय विलयन में विद्युत क्षेत्र के अंतर्गत आयनिक गतिशीलता जलयोजित त्रिज्या के व्युत्क्रमानुपाती होती है:
\[\mu \propto \frac{1}{r_{\text{hydrated}}}\]
अतः सबसे बड़ी जलयोजित त्रिज्या होने के कारण \(\mathrm{Li^+}\) की आयनिक गतिशीलता सबसे कम होती है।</p>
<p><strong>अंतिम संकल्पनात्मक निष्कर्ष:</strong><br/>
जलीय विलयन में लिथियम (\(\mathrm{Li}\)) की आयनिक गतिशीलता सबसे कम होती है।</p>]]></text>
