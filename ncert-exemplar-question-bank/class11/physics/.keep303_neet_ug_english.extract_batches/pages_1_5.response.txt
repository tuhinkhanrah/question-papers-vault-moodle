  <questiontext format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p>A ball is bouncing elastically with a speed \(1\text{ m/s}\) between walls of a railway compartment of size \(10\text{ m}\) in a direction perpendicular to walls. The train is moving at a constant velocity of \(10\text{ m/s}\) parallel to the direction of motion of the ball. As seen from the ground,</p>]]></text></questiontext>
  <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Given Data and Conditions</strong><br/>Speed of the ball relative to the train compartment \(= 1\text{ m/s}\).
Distance between the walls \(= 10\text{ m}\).
Constant velocity of the train relative to the ground \(= +10\text{ m/s}\).</p><p><strong>Step 2: Assumptions and Sign Conventions</strong><br/>Take the direction of the train's motion as positive. Since the train moves at constant velocity, it forms an inertial reference frame.</p><p><strong>Step 3: Relevant Formulae and Principles</strong><br/>By Galilean velocity transformation:
\[v_{\text{ball, ground}} = v_{\text{ball, train}} + v_{\text{train, ground}}\]
Since \(v_{\text{train, ground}}\) is constant, acceleration is invariant:
\[a_{\text{ball, ground}} = a_{\text{ball, train}}\]</p><p><strong>Step 4: Step-by-Step Working</strong><br/>In the train's frame, the time between consecutive wall collisions is:
\[\Delta t = \frac{10\text{ m}}{1\text{ m/s}} = 10\text{ s}\]
As seen from the ground:
- When the ball moves in the direction of the train, its velocity is \(10 + 1 = +11\text{ m/s}\).
- When the ball moves opposite to the train (in the train's frame), its velocity relative to the ground is \(10 - 1 = +9\text{ m/s}\).
Both velocities are positive, so the direction of motion of the ball relative to the ground never changes. However, its speed alternates between \(11\text{ m/s}\) and \(9\text{ m/s}\) every \(10\text{ s}\).
Over any \(20\text{ s}\) interval (one complete back-and-forth period), the ball spends \(10\text{ s}\) at \(11\text{ m/s}\) and \(10\text{ s}\) at \(9\text{ m/s}\), so the total distance is \(11(10) + 9(10) = 200\text{ m}\), giving a fixed average speed of \(\frac{200\text{ m}}{20\text{ s}} = 10\text{ m/s}\).
Because the train moves with constant velocity, the acceleration of the ball as seen from the ground is identical to that seen from the train.</p><p><strong>Step 5: Check and Final Answer</strong><br/>Thus, as seen from the ground, the speed of the ball changes every 10 seconds, the average speed over any 20 second interval is fixed, and the acceleration is the same as from the train.</p>]]></text></generalfeedback>
