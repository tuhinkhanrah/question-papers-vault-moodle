    <generalfeedback format="html"><text><![CDATA[<p><strong>Step 1: Evaluating Assertion (A)</strong><br/>Unlike the carbonates of other alkali metals (\(\mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{CO}_3\), \(\mathrm{K}_2\mathrm{CO}_3\), etc.), which are thermally very stable, lithium carbonate (\(\mathrm{Li}_2\mathrm{CO}_3\)) decomposes readily on heating:<br/>\(\mathrm{Li}_2\mathrm{CO}_3(s) \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{Li}_2\mathrm{O}(s) + \mathrm{CO}_2(g)\).<br/>Thus, Assertion (A) is correct.</p><p><strong>Step 2: Evaluating Reason (R)</strong><br/>Because the \(\mathrm{Li}^+\) ion is very small, it has high polarising power and strongly distorts the electron cloud of the large \(\mathrm{CO}_3^{2-}\) anion. Moreover, \(\mathrm{Li}_2\mathrm{O}\) has a much higher lattice energy than \(\mathrm{Li}_2\mathrm{CO}_3\), making \(\mathrm{Li}_2\mathrm{O}\) and \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) thermodynamically more stable upon heating.
